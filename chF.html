Currency Profiles

Know the instrument before you trade it. Trader's wisdom

What is here

  • One profile for each of the eight major currencies: who controls it, what moves it, when it moves, what it moves with.
  • A pair builder: pick two currencies and see what really drives the pair.
  • A comparison table you can download as CSV.

●F.1 How to read a profile

A currency pair is two economies and two central banks. Before you trade a pair, you should be able to say in one sentence what each side is driven by right now. The profiles give you the starting point. The current story always comes from the calendar and the news.

  1. Character: haven, funding, commodity or "growth" currency.
  2. Central bank: mandate, how often it meets, what it watches.
  3. Key data: the releases that move it most, in Sofia time.
  4. Correlates with: the markets it usually moves with or against.
  5. Traps: the specific ways this currency hurts traders.
Mentor's note

Times are approximate and shift when the US, Europe and Australia change clocks on different dates. Shares of trading come from the BIS Triennial Survey of April 2025, where each trade counts two currencies, so the total is 200%. Always check the calendar for the week.

Funding currency · финансираща валутаA low-rate currency borrowed to buy higher-yielding assets.
Commodity currency · суровинна валутаA currency of an economy that lives largely from commodity exports.

●●F.2 The eight majors

US dollar · USD

Character
The world's reserve and funding currency. On one side of about 89% of all trades. A haven in a real panic, but also rises when the US grows faster than the rest (the "dollar smile").
Central bank
The Fed. Dual mandate: stable prices (2% PCE) and maximum employment. The FOMC meets 8 times a year. Decision at 21:00 Sofia, press conference at 21:30. Projections and the dot plot four times a year.
Key data
NFP and unemployment (first Friday, 15:30), CPI (15:30), PCE, ISM PMIs (17:00), retail sales, weekly jobless claims (Thursday 15:30).
Correlates with
US 2-year yields (strongly), real yields, risk-off in a panic. Usually opposite to gold and commodities priced in dollars.
Best hours
The London–New York overlap, about 15:00–18:00 Sofia.
Traps
The data at 15:30 hits every USD pair at once: one surprise, many stopped positions. US holidays empty the afternoon. Fed speakers can move the market on any day.

Euro · EUR

Character
The second currency of the world, about 29% of trades. Often the "anti-dollar": EURUSD is the most liquid pair. The euro is also your home currency in Bulgaria since 1 January 2026.
Central bank
The ECB. Mandate: prices, 2% inflation over the medium term. Meets about every six weeks (8 times a year). Decision at 15:15 Sofia, press conference at 15:45.
Key data
Flash HICP inflation (around 12:00), PMIs (around 11:00, Germany at 10:30), the German Ifo and ZEW, German state CPIs on the morning of the flash release.
Correlates with
The 2-year spread between German Bunds and US Treasuries. The Italy–Germany spread (BTP–Bund): when it widens, the euro weakens. Energy prices: Europe imports energy, so expensive gas hurts the euro.
Best hours
The London open (10:00–12:00 Sofia) and the overlap with New York.
Traps
"Sources" stories from ECB officials during the press conference. Low volatility in Asia that tempts you into trades with no follow-through. Political risk in member states (elections, budgets).

Japanese yen · JPY

Character
A haven and a funding currency at the same time, about 17% of trades. Japan is the world's largest creditor: in a panic, Japanese money comes home and the yen rises.
Central bank
The BoJ. 2% inflation. Meets 8 times a year; there is no fixed time for the decision, usually between 05:00 and 07:00 Sofia. The Ministry of Finance, not the BoJ, decides on interventions.
Key data
Tokyo CPI (a lead on national inflation), national CPI, the spring wage talks (shunto), BoJ meeting summaries. Japanese data comes out around 01:30 Sofia.
Correlates with
USDJPY follows the US–Japan yield gap, especially the US 10-year. Risk-off: yen up. Stocks down, yen up, as a rule.
Best hours
Tokyo (02:00–05:00 Sofia) and New York, when US yields move.
Traps
Intervention near levels officials have talked about (Appendix E). Holiday liquidity (Golden Week in early May, New Year). The Tokyo fixing at 09:55 local time (02:55 Sofia) and month-end flows.

British pound · GBP

Character
About 10% of trades. A "growth" currency, sensitive to risk and to trust in UK public finances. Often the most volatile of the European majors.
Central bank
The BoE. 2% CPI. Nine members of the MPC vote, and the vote split (for example 5–4) is news in itself. 8 meetings a year, decision at 14:00 Sofia.
Key data
CPI and especially services inflation, wages and the labour market, GDP. UK data comes out at 09:00 Sofia, before the London open.
Correlates with
Gilt yields, as long as trust holds. When yields and the pound fall together, the market is worried about the state (gilts 2022, Chapter 29).
Best hours
London, 10:00–13:00 Sofia, and the overlap.
Traps
Fiscal events (the budget), politics, a thin Asian session (the 2016 flash crash). GBPJPY moves a lot in pips: size it accordingly.

Swiss franc · CHF

Character
A classic haven, about 6% of trades. A rich country with a surplus, low inflation and a history of a strong currency.
Central bank
The SNB. Inflation between 0% and 2%. Only 4 scheduled decisions a year (March, June, September, December), at 10:30 Sofia. Uses the currency itself as a tool: it buys or sells francs.
Key data
Swiss CPI (09:30 Sofia), the SNB decision and the chairman's comments on the franc.
Correlates with
Risk-off and European stress. EURCHF falls when Europe is worried. Gold and the franc often rise together in crises.
Best hours
London.
Traps
The SNB can surprise between meetings: 15 January 2015 (Chapter 29) is the extreme. Low volatility in EURCHF tempts traders to over-size.

Australian dollar · AUD

Character
A commodity and risk currency, about 6% of trades. A liquid "proxy" for China and global growth.
Central bank
The RBA. Inflation 2–3%. 8 meetings a year since 2024, decision in the early morning Sofia time, followed by a press conference.
Key data
Employment (monthly), CPI, RBA minutes. Chinese data (PMI, trade, GDP) often moves AUD more than Australian data. Releases come out around 02:30–03:30 Sofia.
Correlates with
Iron ore and copper prices, Chinese stocks, global risk (S&P 500). AUDJPY is a common gauge of risk appetite.
Best hours
Sydney and Tokyo (01:00–05:00 Sofia), then the London open.
Traps
Moves on Chinese news at night. Was a favourite carry currency, so it falls hard when carry unwinds (Lehman 2008, the yen in 2019).

Canadian dollar · CAD

Character
A commodity currency tied to oil and to the US economy, about 6% of trades. Roughly three quarters of Canadian exports go to the US.
Central bank
The BoC. 2% inflation, target band 1–3%. 8 decisions a year at 16:45 Sofia.
Key data
Canadian jobs (often the same day and time as NFP, 15:30), CPI (15:30), GDP. US data matters almost as much.
Correlates with
Oil (WTI): oil up, CAD up, USDCAD down. Also US–Canada rate differentials and trade news.
Best hours
New York, 15:30–19:00 Sofia.
Traps
When NFP and Canadian jobs come out together, USDCAD gets two surprises at once. Tariff headlines from Washington. Oil inventories on Wednesday (17:30 Sofia).

New Zealand dollar · NZD

Character
The smallest major, a commodity and risk currency. Often moves with AUD, but with its own story in dairy and housing.
Central bank
The RBNZ. Inflation 1–3%. 7 decisions a year, usually around 03:00–04:00 Sofia, with a press conference.
Key data
Quarterly CPI and jobs, the Global Dairy Trade auctions (every two weeks), Chinese data. New Zealand releases often come out late in the Sofia evening, around 23:45.
Correlates with
AUD, dairy prices, global risk.
Best hours
The Asian session.
Traps
The first market to open on Monday, so it often gaps on weekend news. Lower liquidity means wider spreads outside its own session.
Dollar smile · доларова усмивкаThe idea that the dollar rises in both a panic and a strong US boom, and falls in between.
MPC · Monetary Policy CommitteeThe nine-member committee of the Bank of England that votes on rates.
Shunto · шунтоThe spring wage negotiations in Japan, watched by the BoJ.
GDT · Global Dairy TradeThe dairy auction that drives part of New Zealand's export income.

StudentSo which currency is the "best" to trade?

MentorThe one whose drivers you can name without looking. For most people in Sofia that starts with EUR and USD: the session fits your day and the news is easy to follow. Add a third only when you can tell its story as well as the first two.

Debrief

What you take from Appendix F

  • A pair is two banks and two economies. Know both sides.
  • Havens: USD in a panic, JPY, CHF. Commodity currencies: AUD, CAD, NZD.
  • Each currency has its own hour, its own data and its own traps.
  • Start with the pairs whose story you can tell in one sentence.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

You want to add a second pair to EURUSD. You are choosing between AUDUSD and USDCAD. You trade from 10:00 to 13:00 and from 15:00 to 18:00 Sofia time. This week China publishes weak trade data and oil rises 4% after an OPEC+ decision.

Task: which of the two pairs fits your hours better? What drives each side this week? How would you describe the two pairs in one sentence each?

Sample answer
  1. USDCAD fits better: it is most active in New York, inside the 15:00–18:00 window. AUDUSD is most active in Asia, when you are not trading.
  2. AUDUSD: weak China data weighs on AUD, the USD side depends on US data. USDCAD: higher oil supports CAD, so USDCAD has a reason to fall, unless US data is strong.
  3. "AUDUSD this week is China against the Fed." "USDCAD this week is oil against the Fed." The final choice is mine; the hours point to USDCAD.
