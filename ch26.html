From Macro to a Market Read

When the facts change, I change my mind. What do you do, sir? Attributed to John Maynard Keynes

After this chapter you will be able to

  • separate "which way" (macro) from "where and when" (technicals) and combine them into one verdict: green, yellow or red;
  • build a weekly bias file with probabilities, invalidation and a check of your own forecasts;
  • tell fact, forecast and opinion apart in the same headline;
  • see when two positions are really the same bet.

●28.1 Direction versus place and moment

Part III gave you a lot of macro. Now it is important not to misuse it. Macro answers the question "which way is the wind more likely to blow this week?". The technical analysis from Part II answers "where exactly and when do I enter, and where am I wrong?". If you mix up the two questions, you will enter on news with no level, or on levels against the whole world.

Macro says

EUR is weaker than USD this week, probability about 55%.

Macro does not say

Where to enter, where the stop is, when to exit.

Technicals say

The H4 structure is down, liquidity is above 1.1269, the entry zone is here.

Technicals do not say

That tomorrow at 15:30 there is a CPI that can wipe out everything.

The two come together in a verdict. Try every combination.

Clear bias (conviction 4–5)GreenYellow: wait for a breakRed
Lean (conviction 3)Yellow: smaller sizeYellowRed

Bias · пристрастиеYour prior judgement of the more likely direction. It is not a position.
HTF · по-висок таймфреймHigher timeframe: D1, H4. That is where structure comes from.
Conviction · увереностHow strongly macro, technicals and price behaviour point the same way.

●●28.2 The weekly bias file

Everything from this part comes together in one document that is written on Sunday and updated every morning. It is not a diary for feelings and not a list of trades. It is the map you enter the week with. A good bias file has nine parts.

  1. A three-line summary. What matters most this week and why.
  2. Macro backdrop. The regime (growth × inflation), rates, which central banker is a hawk.
  3. Calendar. From Forex Factory, only high-impact events, with Sofia times.
  4. Currency matrix. For each currency: bias with probability, main driver, invalidation, conviction 1–5.
  5. Intermarket links. Gold, oil, yields, indices: do they confirm the picture?
  6. Scenarios. Two or three outcomes with rough probabilities and how each would look on the chart.
  7. Ranking by conviction. Which ideas are cleanest and what threatens them.
  8. Risk watch. What kills the picture (the falsifier) and which positions are really one bet.
  9. Review. Last week: how many scenarios came true, how many did not, how many did not trigger.

Mentor's note

Notice three habits in this file. First, nowhere does it say "EUR will fall": it says "~55%". The gap between 55 and 100 is where the stops live. Second, every idea has a sentence on "what kills it". Third, the file has a note "the data do not match": different sources give different oil prices. An analyst who does not notice this will sooner or later trade a wrong number.

●28.3 The Sunday ritual

The bias file is not written on Monday at 9:55, five minutes before London. It is written on Sunday, calmly, and updated in the morning in 15–20 minutes.

Sunday · 1Review of last weekMark every scenario: came true, wrong or did not trigger. No excuses.
Sunday · 2Forex FactoryThe calendar for the week, filtered to high impact and your currencies only. Write down the Sofia times.
Sunday · 3The matrixRates, the latest data, central bank tone. Probability and conviction for each currency.
Sunday · 4The chartsWeekly and daily structure. Where the liquidity and the zones are. Do they match the matrix?
Sunday · 5Scenarios and risksWhat I expect, what would surprise me, what kills everything.
Every morningUpdateWhat happened overnight, what data come out today, does anything in the matrix change? If not, leave it alone.

StudentSounds like a lot of work for a 100-euro account.

MentorThe account is small. The habit is not. Funds do not look at how much you made from 100 euros, but at whether you can repeat the process with 100,000. Besides, an hour on Sunday is cheaper than three impulsive trades on Tuesday.

Morning update · сутрешно обновяванеA short check before the session: new data, overnight news, changes to the map.

●●28.4 Fact, forecast, opinion

The news flow mixes three different things in one sentence. A fact is something that has already happened and can be checked. A forecast is a claim about the future, usually with a number. An opinion is an interpretation. The market trades facts against forecasts; opinions are noise, unless they belong to someone who votes on rates.

●●●28.5 Positioning: who is already in

Good macro is not enough if everyone has already bought it. When a view is shared by almost everyone, there is no one left to buy, and a small surprise triggers a fast exit through the same door. That is a crowded trade.

InstrumentWhat it showsHow to read it
The COT report (CFTC)Speculators' positions in futures, published on Friday for Tuesday's dataAn extreme net position = the risk of a reversal grows
Retail trader sentimentThe percentage long and short at brokersUsually a contrarian indicator: the crowd is often on the wrong side
Options (risk reversals)How much people pay for protection on the upside versus the downsideExpensive protection in one direction = fear in that direction
Price behaviourGood news, yet price does not riseThe news is already in the price
From the notes · CPI, 14 October 2026

Before the data retail traders are long gold (about 68%), the pound (about 71%) and the euro (about 66%), and even added to those positions the day before. CPI comes out hot, the dollar jumps, and gold, the pound and the euro fall. The crowd is caught on the wrong side in exactly the scenario the file had described in advance.

Crowded trade · претъпкана сделкаA position that almost everyone already holds.
COT · Commitments of TradersA weekly report from the US regulator CFTC on futures positions.
Priced in · в ценатаThe expected event is already reflected in the exchange rate.

●●28.6 Don't mix horizons and don't bet twice

HorizonQuestionToolsMistake
Week (W1/D1)Which way does the wind blow?Bias file, central banks, regimeChanging it after every 20-pip move
Day (H4/H1)Where are the zones and the liquidity?Structure, POI, sessionsTrading an H4 zone without looking at the calendar
Entry (M15/M5/M1)When exactly, and where is the stop?Trigger, confirmationMaking macro decisions on M1

The second trap is quieter. You buy GBPUSD because the pound is strong. You buy AUDUSD because the Australian dollar is strong. They look like two ideas. In fact both are short dollar: if the dollar strengthens, you lose twice. Try it.

Net exposure · нетна експозицияThe sum of all bets for and against one currency.
Relative trade · относителна сделкаA bet on a strong versus a weak currency, without the dollar: for example GBP versus EUR.

●●28.7 Check yourself as an analyst

Analysis without a check is opinion. Write down your scenarios every day and grade them in the evening: came true, wrong, did not trigger, not gradable. After a month you will see which topics you are good at and where you are fooling yourself.

Day (from the notes)Came trueWrongOtherLesson written down the same evening
Friday, 16 October803 did not triggerThe dollar ignored hot inflation expectations: the regime is confirmed
Monday, 19 October452 with no forecastRisk-on together with a stronger dollar: yields were driving it, not risk appetite
Tuesday, 20 October641 not gradableAll the misses come from betting against the weak dollar on single news items
Myth

A good analyst is right almost every time.

Reality

A good analyst knows when they are wrong and writes down why. 6 out of 10 with a clear reason for the misses is worth more than 9 out of 10 with no record, because the first can be improved.

Calibration · калибриранеWhether the things you give 60% actually happen about 60% of the time.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 28

  • Macro says which way, technicals say where and when. The verdict combines them: green, yellow, red.
  • The bias file has probabilities, invalidation, scenarios, a falsifier and a review.
  • Label every number: confirmed, derived, unverified.
  • The market trades facts against forecasts. Opinions are noise.
  • A crowded trade is dangerous even with the right macro.
  • Two pairs against the dollar are one bet. Check your net exposure.

No single right answer

Your Sunday file says: EUR leaning down, ~55%, conviction 3, driver the French spread. Tuesday, London. EURUSD makes a clean break up through the H1 structure and takes the liquidity above yesterday's high. An ECB member speaks at 15:00. You already hold a short GBPJPY and are thinking of buying AUDUSD.

Task: what is the verdict for EURUSD and why? Does the H1 break change the weekly bias? What does your net exposure say? What will you write down in the evening, whatever happens?

Sample answer
  1. Red for EURUSD shorts: the macro is only a lean (conviction 3), and the H1 technicals point the other way. Yellow if I am looking to buy: the technicals are "for", but the macro is "against" and there is a speaker at 15:00. The most common sensible conclusion is not to trade EURUSD until the two agree.
  2. An H1 break does not change the weekly bias. It would change if the invalidation from the file triggered (for example an H4 break) or if the driver changed (the French spread narrowed). I log the break as a warning.
  3. A short GBPJPY is a bet against the pound and for the yen; buying AUDUSD is a bet against the dollar. They do not overlap directly, but both are sensitive to risk sentiment: in risk-off GBPJPY gains and AUDUSD loses. It is important to know this before adding.
  4. In the evening: did the EUR scenario come true, was the H1 break a liquidity trap or the start of a reversal, and did I respect the verdict. The decision whether to trade remains mine; the entry is mandatory.
