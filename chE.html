Case Library

The four most dangerous words in investing are: this time it's different. Attributed to Sir John Templeton

After this appendix you will be able to

  • recognise four families of market accidents: broken promises, liquidity holes, funding squeezes and words that move markets;
  • name what was visible before each event and what was not;
  • turn every case into one rule for your own trading plan;
  • keep your own case archive in the same format.

●E.1 How to use this library

Chapter 29 told five cases in six steps: background, headline, price, why, visible beforehand, lesson. This appendix adds nine more, grouped by mechanism rather than by date. The dates change. The mechanisms come back.

Broken promise

An official level or policy that "will hold" stops holding. The move is fast because years of positions relied on it.

Liquidity hole

Nobody is there to take the other side. Price falls through empty space, then often comes back.

Funding squeeze

Everyone needs the same currency at the same time, usually dollars or yen, to repay debt.

Words and votes

A speech, a referendum or a policy reversal changes the probabilities at once.

Mentor's note

Levels are rounded and come from public reporting. Different platforms printed different lows during fast moves, sometimes far apart. For the lesson, the sequence matters more than the exact pip.

Tail event · опашково събитиеA rare move far outside the normal daily range.

●●E.2 Broken promises

The pound is forced out of the ERM

Background
In October 1990 the UK joins the European Exchange Rate Mechanism at 2.95 Deutsche Marks per pound, with a ±6% band: the floor is about 2.78. German rates are high after reunification, while the UK is in recession and needs lower rates. Holding the band means importing German policy the British economy does not want.
The headline
16 September 1992. In the morning the Bank of England raises its rate from 10% to 12%. In the afternoon it announces 15%. The selling does not stop. At 19:00 London time the Chancellor announces that the UK leaves the ERM.
What price did
The pound floats and falls more than 10% against the mark within weeks. The 15% rate is never applied, and rates are back at 10% the next day.
Why
A rate hike to defend a currency in a recession is not credible: the market knows the government cannot keep it. Speculators sell pounds against a bank that must buy them at the floor. George Soros's fund is reported to make about £1 bn.
Visible beforehand
The policy conflict: a weak economy tied to a strong neighbour's high rates. Pressure on other ERM currencies (Italy, Sweden, Finland) in the same weeks.
Lesson
When a central bank must hurt its own economy to keep a promise, the market tests how much pain it will take. The answer is usually: less than it says.

China surprises with a devaluation

Background
For years the yuan was managed in a tight range against the dollar. The dollar strengthened through 2014–2015, dragging the yuan up with it, while Chinese growth and exports slowed.
The headline
11 August 2015: the People's Bank of China cuts the daily reference rate (the fixing) by about 1.9%, the biggest one-day cut in two decades, and says the fixing will follow the market more closely.
What price did
The yuan loses about 3% in three days, a huge move for a managed currency. Commodity currencies (AUD, NZD) and emerging markets fall. Two weeks later, on 24 August, global stocks drop sharply ("Black Monday" in China) and USDJPY falls from around 124 to around 116 at the low as the yen is bought as a haven.
Why
A small step in a currency everyone thought was "stable" changes the question from "how much" to "how much more". It also signals that China's economy is weaker than the official numbers suggested.
Visible beforehand
A strong dollar, weak Chinese exports, falling commodity prices, the IMF discussing the yuan's role in its reserve basket.
Lesson
A managed currency stores up pressure. When the manager lets go a little, the move travels through every currency linked to that economy, starting with AUD.

Japan buys yen for the first time since 1998

Background
2022: the Fed hikes fast, the BoJ keeps rates below zero and caps the 10-year yield. The rate gap widens every month and USDJPY rises from about 115 in March to about 145 in September.
The headline
22 September 2022, hours after the BoJ keeps its policy unchanged: Japan's Ministry of Finance sells dollars and buys yen, the first such intervention since 1998. On 21 October, near 152, it intervenes again without announcing it at first.
What price did
September: from about 145.9 to about 140.3 within hours. October: from about 151.9 to about 146 within an hour or two, late in the New York session. Both times part of the move comes back in the following days.
Why
An intervention buys time. It does not change the rate gap. As long as the BoJ is not hiking, the reason for the trend is still there. The trend finally turns in November 2022, when US inflation starts to cool and US yields fall.
Visible beforehand
Verbal warnings from the Ministry of Finance that grow sharper ("we will act decisively"), a "rate check" with banks, round numbers like 145 and 150 discussed in the press.
Lesson
Near a level the authorities have talked about, size down or stand aside. Intervention moves 400–500 pips without warning, and stops do not care that you were right about the trend.
ERM · Exchange Rate MechanismThe European system of bands between currencies before the euro.
Fixing · фиксингAn official daily reference rate set by a central bank.
Intervention · интервенцияA government or central bank buying or selling its own currency in the market.
Rate check · проверка на курсаAuthorities asking banks for quotes, often a last warning before an intervention.

●●E.3 Liquidity holes

Two minutes in the Asian session

Background
October 2016, three months after the Brexit vote. The pound is already weak and the government has just signalled a "hard" Brexit. The market is short pounds and nervous.
The headline
There is none. 7 October 2016, around 08:07 Tokyo time (02:07 in Sofia), the quietest part of the day for the pound: London and New York are closed.
What price did
GBPUSD falls from about 1.26 to about 1.15 on the main data feeds within two minutes, and even lower on some platforms. About twenty minutes later it is back around 1.24. Spreads widen hundreds of times.
Why
Investigations never found one single cause. The likely mix: very thin liquidity at that hour, stops and option hedges triggered one after another, and algorithms that stopped quoting as the move accelerated. Each sale pushed price into the next cluster of stops.
Visible beforehand
The time of day and the one-sided positioning. Not the event itself.
Lesson
A stop placed in a thin hour can be filled far from your level. If you hold positions overnight, size them for the gap, not for the stop.

The yen jumps while Japan is on holiday

Background
3 January 2019. Japanese banks are closed for the New Year holiday. Stocks are falling after Apple warns about weak sales in China. Japanese retail traders hold large long positions in high-yield currencies against the yen, especially AUDJPY and TRYJPY.
The headline
Again none. In the early hours of the Asian session (around 00:30–00:40 in Sofia) the yen rises about 3% against the dollar within about 30 seconds.
What price did
Within minutes AUD falls about 7% against the yen and about 3% against the dollar, briefly touching a 10-year low near 0.67. Spreads go from about 2 pips to more than 100. Half the move comes back within minutes, most of it within the hour.
Why
Thin holiday liquidity met crowded retail positioning. As the yen rose, automatic stop-outs on retail accounts bought more yen, which triggered the next stop-outs. Market-making algorithms switched off until banks stepped in.
Visible beforehand
The holiday, the crowded yen shorts among Japanese retail traders, a risk-off mood in stocks.
Lesson
Holidays plus crowded positioning plus a thin hour is the recipe. In the first days of January, the week between Christmas and New Year and around Japanese holidays, a scalper trades smaller or not at all.
Flash crash · светкавичен сривA very fast fall and recovery, usually in minutes, often with no news.
Stop cascade · каскада от стоповеStops triggered one after another, each pushing price into the next.

●●●E.4 Funding squeezes

The crisis starts in the US, and the dollar rises

Background
Mid-2008: commodities at records, AUD near parity at about 0.98, AUDJPY above 100. The carry trade is crowded: borrow yen at almost zero, buy Australian and New Zealand dollars at 7–8%. European banks hold huge dollar assets funded with short-term dollar borrowing.
The headline
15 September 2008: Lehman Brothers files for bankruptcy. Short-term dollar funding markets freeze.
What price did
AUDUSD falls from about 0.98 in July to about 0.60 at the end of October. AUDJPY roughly halves, from above 100 to about 55. The dollar and the yen are the two strongest currencies, even though the crisis began in the US.
Why
Two forced flows at once. Banks outside the US need dollars to repay dollar debts, so they buy dollars at any price. Carry traders must repay yen loans, so they buy yen. Fundamentals of "who caused the crisis" do not matter when everyone owes the same currency.
Resolution
The Fed opens unlimited dollar swap lines with major central banks in October 2008. That calms dollar funding, but the risk-off continues into early 2009.
Visible beforehand
Record carry positions, rising credit spreads since 2007, the failure of Bear Stearns in March 2008.
Lesson
In a real panic the currency people owe rises, not the "healthier" one. Watch funding stress, not just the headlines about who is to blame.

The dash for dollars

Background
Late February 2020: the virus spreads in Europe. On 6 March OPEC+ talks collapse and oil starts a price war. Stocks and credit sell off.
The headline
A chain: emergency Fed cut of 0.5 pp (3 March), another to 0–0.25% plus QE (Sunday 15 March), wider dollar swap lines (15 and 19 March), "unlimited" QE (23 March).
What price did
The broad dollar rises more than 8% in two weeks after 6 March to a record high. AUDUSD falls to about 0.55, its lowest in about 17 years. Gold first falls by more than 10% as people sell what they can to raise cash, then rallies to new highs by summer. Oil loses almost two thirds in the quarter.
Why
The world has trillions of dollar debts. When income stops, everyone needs dollars at once. Even safe assets like gold and Treasuries are sold to raise them.
Resolution
Once the Fed floods the system with dollars through swap lines and QE, the dollar peaks around 20–23 March and falls for the rest of the year.
Visible beforehand
The 2008 template: crowded risk positions, dollar debt worldwide, a sudden stop in activity.
Lesson
In the first phase of a panic correlations go to one: everything except the dollar falls. The turn usually comes from the central bank, not from the data.
Swap line · суап линияAn agreement through which a central bank lends its currency to another central bank.
Dollar funding · доларово финансиранеBorrowing dollars short-term to hold dollar assets.
Dash for cash · бягство към кешSelling everything, even safe assets, to raise cash.

●●E.5 Words and votes

Three words from Mario Draghi

Background
Summer 2012: the euro area debt crisis. Spanish 10-year yields above 7%, Italian ones above 6%. The market openly bets on a break-up of the euro. EURUSD falls to about 1.20 on 24 July.
The headline
26 July 2012, London: ECB President Mario Draghi says the ECB "is ready to do whatever it takes to preserve the euro. And believe me, it will be enough."
What price did
EURUSD rises more than 100 pips the same day and keeps climbing for months. Spanish and Italian yields fall sharply. The euro is near 1.30 by September, when the ECB announces the OMT bond-buying programme.
Why
The market bet on the euro breaking because nobody stood behind it. A credible promise of unlimited support removes that bet. OMT is never actually used: the promise is enough.
Visible beforehand
Extreme short positioning against the euro, yields at levels that could not last, political pressure on the ECB to act.
Lesson
A credible "whatever it takes" from a central bank with unlimited money is the opposite of a broken promise. The difference is whether the bank can print the currency it is defending.

The referendum night

Background
23 June 2016: the UK votes on EU membership. Polls are close but betting markets give "Remain" around 75–80%. The pound rallies into the vote, to about 1.50 as polls close.
The headline
Results come in overnight, region by region. Early results from the north of England are much stronger for "Leave" than expected.
What price did
GBPUSD falls from about 1.50 to about 1.32 within hours, its biggest one-day fall in modern history. EURGBP and GBPJPY move even more. The yen and gold rise.
Why
The price had the "Remain" result baked in. The surprise was not only the result, but how far it was from what was priced. The move happened while results came in, at night, with thin liquidity.
Visible beforehand
The date and the binary risk were known weeks in advance. Option prices showed record demand for protection against a fall.
Lesson
On a known binary event you do not need to guess the result. You need to be out, or small, before it. The cost of missing the move is nothing compared with being on the wrong side of a 1,800-pip night.
OMT · Outright Monetary TransactionsThe ECB programme for buying the bonds of countries under pressure, announced in 2012.
Binary event · бинарно събитиеAn event with two outcomes and a big gap between them.

  1. Crowded positioning is in almost every case. The news is the match, positioning is the fuel.
  2. Thin liquidity turns a move into a crash: night, holidays, the gap between New York and Tokyo.
  3. A promise that costs the promiser pain does not last. A promise backed by a printing press usually does.
  4. In a panic, the currency people owe goes up: the dollar, then the yen.
  5. The first move is often partly reversed in liquidity accidents and interventions, but not in regime changes like 1992 or 2008.
For a scalper

Almost none of these cases could be traded at the moment of the event. Spreads were huge and fills random. The edge was in the step before: smaller size, no positions over a known binary event, no tight stops in thin hours.

Regime change · смяна на режимаA shift after which old relationships and levels no longer apply.
Debrief

What you take from Appendix E

  • Four families: broken promises, liquidity holes, funding squeezes, words and votes.
  • Crowded positioning is the common fuel. Thin liquidity is the accelerator.
  • In a panic the dollar and the yen rise, whoever "caused" the crisis.
  • Near intervention levels and over binary events: smaller, or out.
  • Every case ends with one rule for your plan.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

It is 27 December. Liquidity is thin. USDJPY has risen for six months because of the rate gap and is three big figures below the level where the Ministry of Finance intervened last year. Officials say they are "watching with a high sense of urgency". COT shows record yen shorts. You see a clean bullish setup on M15 with a 12-pip stop.

Task: which cases from this library does this combine? What could go wrong with the trade even if the setup is good? What would you change, if anything, in size, timing or instrument?

Sample answer
  1. Yen 2022 (intervention near a talked-about level), Yen 2019 (holiday liquidity and crowded positioning) and the yen part of Lehman 2008 (crowded carry).
  2. The setup can be right and the trade still lose 400+ pips of slippage if the Ministry acts. A 12-pip stop in a thin hour can fill 50–100 pips away.
  3. Reasonable options: skip it, cut the size to a fraction of normal risk, or trade a pair without yen. Waiting until liquidity returns in January also counts as a decision. The choice is mine, but the asymmetric risk is clear.
