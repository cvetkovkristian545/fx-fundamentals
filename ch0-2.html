Absolute Zero: Markets, Buyers, Sellers

The first lesson of economics is scarcity: there is never enough of anything to fully satisfy all those who want it. Thomas Sowell, “Is Reality Optional?” (1993)

After this chapter you will be able to

  • explain to someone what a market, bid, ask and spread are, using a market stall as the example;
  • show why price rises when demand exceeds supply;
  • say in plain words what liquidity is and why price “goes” where the orders are.

●0.2.1 What a market is

A market is a place where buyers and sellers agree on a price. It can be a tomato stall in an old-town square, an exchange in New York or a server in London matching millions of orders a second. The logic is the same everywhere.

The buyer wants to pay as little as possible. The seller wants to get as much as possible. A trade happens only when both agree on one price. That price is the last agreement between them. It is the price you see on the chart.

Mentor's note

Price is not the market's opinion of what something is worth. Price is the point where the last buyer and the last seller stopped arguing.

Market · пазарAny place, physical or electronic, where buyers and sellers meet.
Buyer · купувачThe side that wants to acquire something. In trading: long.
Seller · продавачThe side that wants to get rid of something. In trading: short.

●0.2.2 Bid, ask and spread: the euro stall

Imagine a stall on the corner that exchanges dollars for euros. The sign shows two prices:

Bid is the highest price someone is willing to pay right now. Ask (or offer) is the lowest price at which someone is willing to sell right now. If you want to buy immediately, you pay the ask. If you want to sell immediately, you get the bid. The difference is the spread.

On EURUSD the spread is often under one pip in active hours. On an exotic pair or in the middle of the night it can be ten times wider. The spread is the first price you pay, before the market has even moved. We will calculate it in Chapter 2.

Bid · цена купуваThe highest price a buyer is offering right now.
Ask · цена продаваThe lowest price at which a seller is offering right now.
Spread · спредAsk minus bid. The cost of getting in and out instantly.

●0.2.3 Supply and demand: why prices move

Bread gets more expensive when there is less wheat (supply falls) or when more people want bread (demand rises). Petrol gets more expensive when OPEC cuts output or when everyone heads to the seaside in summer. The euro gets more expensive when more people around the world want euros, for example because the European Central Bank pays a higher interest rate than others.

That is all of economics in one line: when demand exceeds supply, price rises; when supply exceeds demand, price falls. Price moves until it finds the point where the quantity people want to buy equals the quantity others want to sell. That point is called equilibrium.

StudentSo if I know whether demand will rise, I know where price will go?

MentorIf you knew that for certain, you would. But nobody knows it for certain. That is why this book talks about probabilities, not predictions. All analysis is an attempt to judge which side is more likely to win.

Demand · търсенеHow much of something people want and are able to buy at a given price.
Supply · предлаганеHow much of something people want and are able to sell at a given price.
Equilibrium · равновесиеThe price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.

●0.2.4 The same idea on the chart

On the chart you don't see the supply and demand curves. You see their footprints. When price falls to a level and bounces sharply, there were more buyers than sellers there. Traders call such a place a demand zone. When price rises to a level and turns down sharply, there were more sellers there: a supply zone.

When one side is so much stronger that price skips levels with almost no trades, an imbalance is left behind. On the chart it looks like a big candle followed by a “gap” between the wicks of the neighbouring candles. The market often comes back to fill these gaps. In Chapter 7 we will call them FVGs.

Demand zone · зона на търсенеA price area from which buyers pushed price higher.
Supply zone · зона на предлаганеA price area from which sellers pushed price lower.
Imbalance · дисбалансA fast move in which one side found almost no opposing orders.

●●0.2.5 Liquidity in plain words

Liquidity means: how easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. At a stall with one seller and three crates of tomatoes, liquidity is low. If you buy all three crates, the next buyer has to wait or pay more. In a market with a thousand sellers, liquidity is high.

Try it. Below is the euro “order book”: who wants to sell how much and at what price. Press “Buy 1 million” a few times and watch what happens to the price.

See? When buyers eat all the sell orders at one price, the next trade happens at a higher price. Price moves because the liquidity at one level runs out.

Now the most important idea in the whole book, which you will meet again and again. Big players (banks, funds) need a lot of liquidity to get in with huge sums. It is stacked where many people have left orders: above obvious highs and below obvious lows, where other traders' stops sit. That is why price often goes there first, takes those orders and only then sets off in its real direction. In Chapter 7 this will be called a sweep.

Myth

Price moves because it is “cheap” or “expensive”.

Reality

Price moves because one side is more aggressive and eats the other side's orders. “Cheap” and “expensive” are opinions. Orders are facts.

Liquidity · ликвидностThe presence of orders you can trade against without moving the price much.
Order book · книга на поръчкитеA list of resting buy and sell orders by price.
Stop loss · стопAn order that closes a trade at a loss at a set price. Piles of stops in one place are liquidity.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 0.2

  • Price is the last agreement between a buyer and a seller.
  • You buy at the ask and sell at the bid. The spread is the difference and it is your cost.
  • More demand than supply raises price. More supply than demand lowers it.
  • On the chart, supply and demand leave zones and imbalances.
  • Liquidity is the presence of orders. Price moves when the liquidity at a level runs out, and it often goes first to where the stops are.

No single right answer

EURUSD has traded around 1.0850 for three days. Two highs at almost the same price, 1.0890, and one low at 1.0820 are clearly visible. The news is that the ECB will speak tomorrow.

Task: where do you think liquidity has built up? What might price do before it picks a direction? How does the ECB news affect demand for euros?

Sample answer
  1. Above the two highs at 1.0890 there are probably many sellers' stops and buyers' orders waiting for a breakout. Below 1.0820 sit the buyers' stops.
  2. Price may go above 1.0890, take those orders and come back, or do the same below 1.0820. That is a sweep (Chapter 7).
  3. If the ECB sounds more hawkish (higher rates for longer), demand for euros will probably rise. If it sounds softer, the opposite.
  4. Before the speech: watch, don't act. I expect a move towards one of the two pools of liquidity and I watch where price fails to stay.
