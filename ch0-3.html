Start Here: the Fast Track and Your First Backtest

Amateurs practise until they get it right. Professionals practise until they can't get it wrong. Trader's wisdom

After this chapter you will be able to

  • follow a short path through the book instead of reading 41 chapters in a row;
  • write one simple setup as a set of yes/no rules;
  • backtest it on 100 trades, read the numbers honestly and decide whether it has earned real money;
  • add liquidity, SMC and order flow later, one piece at a time, and prove that each piece helps.

●0.3.1 This book is a library, not a to-do list

FX Fundamentals has 41 chapters and seven appendices. You don't need all of them before your first trade. You need a small core that lets you run one setup cleanly: read the chart, find the level, size the position, respect the news, manage the trade and write it down.

Everything else, like liquidity sweeps, order blocks, order flow and the dollar system, is a refinement. A refinement only has value if it improves something that already works. If you add it to a process that doesn't exist yet, you just get more ways to be confused.

So the book has three layers:

  1. The core: enough to take one well-defined trade with correct risk.
  2. The proof: a backtest that shows whether that trade has a positive expectancy.
  3. The refinements: SMC, order flow and macro, added one at a time and tested again.
Mentor's note

Most beginners do it backwards. They learn twenty concepts, mix them into one chart and then wonder why nothing is repeatable. One simple setup that you have tested is worth more than ten clever ones you haven't.

Setup · сетъпA trade idea written as fixed rules: where, when, entry, stop, target.
Refinement · надгражданеAn extra filter added to a setup that already works, to improve its numbers.

●0.3.3 Write your setup on one card

A backtest can only test rules, not feelings. "I enter when it looks strong" can't be tested. "I enter when an M15 candle closes back above the level after taking out the previous low" can. Fill in each field with a rule you could hand to a stranger. The card is saved in this browser.

Myth

I'll keep it flexible and use my judgement on each trade.

Reality

Judgement comes after hundreds of trades with fixed rules. Before that, "flexible" usually means "changing the rules after you see the result".

Trigger · тригерThe exact event that turns a possible trade into an entry.
Invalidation · отмянаThe price at which the idea is wrong. Your stop goes there, not at a round number of pips.

●●0.3.5 How to backtest, step by step

  1. Freeze the card. Copy your setup card into the journal. You are not allowed to change it until the test is over. A change means a new test from trade 1.
  2. One pair, one session. EURUSD is the running example of this book. One pair means you learn its rhythm instead of averaging ten different ones.
  3. Pick a long, mixed period. Start at least 6–12 months back. You need trending months and ranging months, quiet weeks and news weeks.
  4. Mark the context first. At the start of each day in replay, mark the H1 structure and the key levels, exactly as your card says. Only then move forward.
  5. Move one candle at a time. Never jump ahead "to see what happens". The moment you look at the future, that day's trades are contaminated.
  6. Log every valid setup. Including the ones you "wouldn't have taken". Skipping trades you don't like is the fastest way to fake a good result.
  7. Record in R. A full stop is −1R. A full target is +3R. Partials and breakeven exits are recorded as they happened (+1.2R, 0R). Subtract costs: spread and commission are roughly 0.05–0.15R per trade for a typical intraday stop.
  8. Screenshot entry and exit. In 0.3.8 you will re-sort these screenshots to find what a refinement could fix.
  9. Respect the news rules. Check the past economic calendar for each day. If your card says "no trade around red news", skip those setups as you would live.
  10. Stop at 100 trades. Then read the numbers in 0.3.6 and only then judge.
Mentor's note

Backtesting is boring. That's the point. Live trading is the same boring routine, with money attached. If you can't sit through 100 replayed trades, the live market will find that out much faster and much more expensively.

Hindsight bias · заблуда на задната датаSeeing the "obvious" trade only because you already know what happened next.
R-multiple · R-кратноThe result of a trade measured in units of the initial risk.

●●0.3.6 The backtest lab: read your numbers

Paste your results in R, in the order they happened, separated by commas or spaces. For example: 3, -1, -1, 2.4, -1, 0, 3. The lab works out the statistics and checks them against the gates in 0.3.7.

Two numbers matter more than the rest. Expectancy is the average result per trade, after costs. The range of the win rate tells you how sure you can be. With 100 trades and a 35% win rate, the true win rate could plausibly be anywhere from about 26% to 44%. With 30 trades the range is twice as wide. That is why 30 trades prove almost nothing (Chapter 5).

Expectancy · очакванеAverage result per trade: win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss.
Profit factor · профит факторTotal won ÷ total lost. Above 1 means the setup made money.
Max drawdown · максимален спадThe deepest fall from a peak of the equity curve, here in R.

●●0.3.7 The gates: has it earned real money?

GatePassWhy
Sample≥ 100 tradesBelow that, luck and skill look the same.

Max drawdown≤ 10RAt 1% risk that is about a 10% drop, the usual prop-firm limit (Ch. 33).
Longest losing streakOne you accept in advanceIf 9 losses in a row would make you quit, you'll quit live too (Ch. 35).

If it passes: forward test on a demo account for 30 trades with the same card (Ch. 32 and 34). Live results are usually a little worse than the backtest. If the demo expectancy is still positive and you followed your rules on at least 90% of trades, go live small, at 0.5–1% risk.

If it fails: that is a result, not a defeat. Look at the screenshots of the losers. Is there a pattern? Maybe they cluster in one session, against the H1 trend, or right after news. That pattern is exactly what a refinement in the next section is for.

Forward test · форуърд тестRunning the same rules on live data, on a demo account or with tiny risk, to check the backtest.

●●●0.3.8 Only now: SMC and order flow, one piece at a time

Now the advanced chapters have a job. Each idea from Chapter 7 or Chapter 15 becomes a filter you can test against your base setup:

  • "Only take the trade if a liquidity sweep of the previous low happened first."
  • "Only enter inside a fair value gap or order block, not just at the level."
  • "Only take it in the London or New York kill zone."
  • "Only when delta or volume confirms at the level" (order flow, Ch. 15).

The method is always the same. Add one filter. Re-run it on your old trades and on at least 50 new ones. Compare expectancy and drawdown with the base version. Keep the filter only if the numbers improve and you still get enough trades. A filter that turns 100 trades into 12 has not improved anything. It has just made the sample too small to judge.

Myth

Advanced concepts give a higher win rate, so I should start with them.

Reality

More conditions usually mean fewer trades and a later entry. Sometimes that helps, sometimes it doesn't. The only way to know is to test it against something simpler.

Common backtest traps

  • Peeking: scrolling ahead, then "deciding" to take the trade.
  • Cherry-picking: testing only the clean, trending months.
  • Rule drift: slowly changing the card halfway through the test.
  • Curve fitting: adding filters until the past looks perfect. It won't look perfect in the future.
  • Forgetting costs: a +0.1R edge before spread is roughly zero after it.
Filter · филтърAn extra condition that must be true before the setup is allowed.
Curve fitting · напасванеTuning rules to the past so tightly that they stop working on new data.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 0.3

  • Learn the core first: stages 1–3. Those chapters are enough to run one setup.
  • Write the setup as rules a stranger could follow, and freeze them during a test.
  • Backtest at least 100 trades, one candle at a time, logging every valid setup in R.
  • Judge by expectancy after costs, the range of the win rate and the drawdown, not by the best week.
  • Add SMC and order flow later, one filter at a time, and keep only what improves the numbers.

No single right answer

A reader backtested a setup on 40 trades: 18 wins at +3R, 22 losses at −1R. They are excited and want to add an order-block filter and go live next week with 2% risk.

Task: what are the numbers really saying? What would you tell them to do next, and in what order?

Sample answer
  1. The raw result looks great: 45% win rate, expectancy 0.45 × 3 − 0.55 × 1 = +0.80R before costs. That is suspiciously high, so first check for peeking or skipped trades.
  2. 40 trades is too few. The plausible win-rate range is roughly 30% to 60%. The setup may be good, but this sample can't show it yet.
  3. Next: keep the card frozen and continue to 100 trades. Then 30 demo trades.
  4. The order-block filter comes after that, tested against the base version on at least 50 trades.
  5. Risk stays at 1% or less. Doubling risk on an unproven edge doubles the cost of being wrong.
