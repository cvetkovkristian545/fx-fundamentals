What Forex Is and Who Moves It

The market is never wrong. Opinions often are. Jesse Livermore (attributed)

After this chapter you will be able to

  • read a quote such as EURUSD 1.0850 and say exactly what it means;
  • list who trades on the currency market and which of them have size and information;
  • say when London and New York are open in Sofia time, summer and winter.

●1.1 The currency pair: one economy against another

Forex (FX, foreign exchange) is the market where one currency is exchanged for another. Currencies always trade in pairs, because you can't “buy a dollar” in a vacuum: you buy dollars by paying with something else, for example euros.

In the quote EURUSD 1.0850 the first currency (EUR) is the base and the second (USD) is the quote currency. The number says how many units of the quote currency one unit of the base costs. In other words: one euro costs 1.0850 dollars.

When EURUSD rises, the euro is getting stronger against the dollar (or the dollar weaker against the euro, which is the same thing). If you buy EURUSD, you are in effect buying euros and selling dollars. That is why every pair is a bet on the relative strength of two economies: their interest rates, growth, inflation and politics. This is the bridge between the chart and Part III.

Mentor's note

When you hear “the dollar is strong”, always ask: against what? A currency is not strong on its own. It is stronger than another one.

●1.2 How big the market is and who moves it

According to the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) survey of April 2025, about 9.6 trillion dollars a day trade on the currency market on average. The dollar is on one side of almost nine out of every ten trades. EURUSD is the most traded pair in the world. That is why it is our running example.

Our edge is not size or information. Our edge is flexibility: we can stay out, wait, enter small and exit fast. A fund with billions can't. A large part of this book is about how to use exactly this edge.

Myth

The banks are “hunting” your stop specifically.

Reality

Nobody sees your account. But when thousands of traders put stops in the same obvious place, that pile of orders is liquidity the big players use to get in. The answer is not to be where everyone is.

●1.3 A market that never sleeps: Asia, London, New York

The currency market runs 24 hours from Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York. The day is handed from one financial centre to the next like a relay: first Sydney and Tokyo, then London, then New York. Each centre has its own behaviour, which we will look at in detail in Chapter 9.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 01

  • EURUSD 1.0850 means one euro costs 1.0850 dollars. Buying EURUSD = buying euros against dollars.
  • Every pair is a comparison of two economies.
  • The market is huge (about $9.6 trillion a day) and is driven by central banks, big banks and funds.
  • The small trader's edge is flexibility, not size.
  • London 10:00–19:00, New York 15:00–24:00 Sofia time; US data comes out at 15:30.

No single right answer

It's Wednesday, 14:50 Sofia time. EURUSD has been in a tight range since the morning. London is open; New York opens in 10 minutes. US CPI comes out at 15:30. A friend asks: “Why isn't price moving? Should I get in now, before it goes?”

Task: explain what is happening in the market right now, who is missing and what is expected in the next hour. What would you watch?

Sample answer
  1. The big US players aren't at their desks yet, and everyone is waiting for CPI. Nobody wants to take risk before the number.
  2. New York opens at 15:00 and CPI comes at 15:30. That is usually where the day's move comes from.
  3. Getting in now means a coin-flip bet before a release. The protocol in Chapter 13 says: no new positions 30 minutes before major news.
  4. I would watch the high and low of the morning range. After CPI I look at which side gets taken and whether price stays beyond it or comes back.
