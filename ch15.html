●●●15.1 The market as an auction

Before this chapter

Order flow is a refinement, not a starting point. On the fast track it comes after your base setup has passed a backtest and a demo test. Then test each order-flow condition as a filter (0.3.8).

In an auction the price rises until buyers stop bidding and falls until sellers stop offering. The market works the same way. There are two modes:

Balance

Price swings inside a range. Both sides agree this is a "fair" price. Most trading happens in the middle.

Imbalance

One side is stronger. Price leaves the range and looks for a new area of agreement. The move is fast, with little trading.

The balance range rules in this framework: the meaningful levels are the top and the bottom of the range. At the bottom you expect buyers, at the top sellers. You do not sell at the bottom unless sellers break it and accept prices below it (volume trades there). You do not buy at the top unless buyers break it and accept prices above it.

AMT · теория на пазарния аукционAuction Market Theory: price seeks a place of agreement between buyers and sellers.
Acceptance · приеманеPrice stays beyond the level and volume trades there.
Rejection · отказPrice quickly returns from the level, with almost no volume beyond it.

●●●15.2 The tools

Important for forex

The spot currency market has no central exchange, so there is no total volume. The volume your broker shows is only its own flow or the tick count. For real order flow, analysts watch the CME futures: 6E for the euro, 6B for the pound, 6J for the yen, GC for gold. They move almost in sync with spot and are a good proxy.

DOMDepth of Market: resting orders by price.
HeatmapThe DOM drawn over time.
FootprintA candle split by price, with bought and sold at each.
Delta / CVDAggressive buys minus aggressive sells; the accumulated delta.
Aggressive vs passiveA market order (aggressive) versus a limit order (passive).

●●●15.3 Volume profile

A volume profile turns volume sideways: it shows how much traded at each price over a period, not per candle. You get "mountains" and "valleys".

HVNHigh volume nodePrice slows down and trades for a long time.

●●●15.4 Absorption: effort versus result

Absorption happens when one side pushes hard (large effort: big negative delta) and price barely moves (small result). Someone big sits with limit orders and takes everything. When the aggressive sellers are exhausted, price usually turns the other way.

This is a divergence between delta and price, a relative of the SMT and RSI divergences from Chapter 4, but on real orders. It is strongest at a key level (range low, PDL, zone after a sweep).

Absorption · абсорбцияPassive orders take in aggressive pressure without price moving.
Effort vs resultWyckoff's principle: big effort with a small result is a warning.

●●15.5 If-then plans and bad conditions

The most valuable practice from the order flow world is not a tool but a habit: arrive at the session with an idea. Before the open you write "if–then" sentences around the levels:

  • If price reaches the bottom of the range and I see strong buying (absorption, positive delta), then I expect a move to yesterday's high.
  • If the bottom breaks and there is acceptance below it, then I expect a move to the next demand zone.
  • If neither happens, then I do nothing today.

When conditions are bad. Before big news the market often "drifts" in a tight range with false breaks. On days before FOMC or early in a week when the market is waiting, breakouts often fail. The calendar is the first filter.

Myth

With Bookmap I will see what the banks are doing and win every time.

Reality

Order flow shows what is happening now, not what will happen. Orders can be bait. Without context and location it is just a lot of flashing colours.

If-then plan · план ако–тогаваConditional sentences around the levels, written before the session.
CLCContext, Location, Confirmation: the three things every good setup has.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 15

  • The market is an auction: balance and imbalance. In a range the meaningful levels are the edges.
  • DOM and heatmap: the passive orders. Footprint, delta, CVD: the aggressive ones.
  • Volume profile: POC, value area, HVN, LVN. LVN ≈ FVG.
  • Absorption: big effort, small result. Strongest at a key level.
  • CLC: context, location, confirmation. Order flow is only the confirmation. For forex: the CME futures.

No single right answer

6E (the euro future) is in a weekly range of 1.0820–1.0870. Yesterday was a balanced day. Today in London price drops to 1.0818, below the range low. The footprint shows a big negative delta at 1.0818–1.0820, but price does not continue lower. CVD makes a lower low, price makes an equal low. The EURUSD D1 is bullish.

Task: apply CLC. What is the name for what you are seeing? Write two "if–then" plans.

Sample answer
  1. Context: D1 bullish. Location: the bottom of the weekly range, with a sweep below it. Confirmation: absorption (big negative delta, no new drop) and a CVD/price divergence.
  2. If price comes back above 1.0820 with positive delta (buyers take the initiative), then I expect a move to the middle of the range and yesterday's high.
  3. If price accepts prices below 1.0818 (volume trades there, CVD keeps falling, no absorption), then the absorption was only a pause and the setup is invalidated.
  4. If neither is clear by the end of the London window, then today is not my day.
