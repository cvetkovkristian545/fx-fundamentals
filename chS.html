Trading Styles: Which One Is Yours

There is no best style. There is a style that fits your hours, your nerves and your account. Trader's wisdom

After this chapter you will be able to

  • tell scalping, day trading, swing and position trading apart by timeframe, holding time and screen time;
  • calculate how much of your risk the spread and commission eat in each style;
  • explain what price action, SMC, ICT, order flow, macro and algorithmic traders read;
  • pick one style, one market and one session for your first 90 days.

●30.1 The four horizons

Style is first a question of time: how long you hold the trade and how many hours you can sit in front of the chart. The same setup, for example the sweep and MSS from Chapter 7, works on M1 and on a daily chart. The difference is how many pips the stop is, how many times a day it appears and how hard costs hit you.

StyleEntry onHoldingTradesScreen timeTypical EURUSD stop
ScalpingM1–M5seconds to 30 minmany a day2–4 h straight3–8 pips
Day tradingM5–M15, H1 contextuntil the end of the day0–3 a day2–5 h in one session10–25 pips
Swing tradingH1–H4, D1 context2 to 10 days1–4 a week30–60 min a day30–100 pips
Position tradingD1–W1weeks to monthsa few a montha few hours a week100–400 pips

The figures are a guide, not a rule. The good news: with 1% risk the position size is calculated from the stop (Chapter 11), so the pips do not change how much you lose when you are wrong. They do change how much of the risk goes on costs.

Myth

"Scalping is the fastest way to big money because you make lots of trades."

Reality

More trades mean more costs, more decisions under pressure and more chances of an execution mistake. Scalping is the least forgiving. For a beginner it is usually the hardest style, not the easiest.

Scalping · скалпиранеVery short trades for a few pips, usually on M1–M5.
Day trading · дневна търговияAll trades are closed within the day, with no overnight positions.
Swing tradingTrades of several days that catch one "swing" in price.
Position tradingLong positions lasting weeks to months, driven mainly by macro.
Swap · суапThe interest you pay or receive for a position held overnight.

●30.2 The schools: what each one reads

The horizon says how long. The school says what you look at to decide. Most traders combine two schools, for example SMC for the entry and macro for the direction.

Classic price actionCandles, levels, trendSupport and resistance, candle patterns, breakout and retest (Chapters 3–4). Simple and transparent. Weakness: lots of subjective levels.
SMCLiquidity and structureBOS, CHoCH, sweep, order block, FVG, premium/discount (Chapters 6–7). Explains why price raids stops. Weakness: it is easy to see a zone everywhere.
ICTTime + liquidityKill zones, OTE, PD arrays, Silver Bullet, Power of 3. Much of the SMC vocabulary comes from here. Weakness: lots of rules, some untestable.
Order flowReal orders and volumeFootprint, DOM, delta, volume profile on futures like ES and NQ (Chapter 15). You see who is pushing. Weakness: you need exchange data and a platform.
Macro FXRates and the economyRate differentials, inflation, central banks, risk-on/off (Part III). Gives the direction for weeks. Weakness: poor for a precise entry.
AlgorithmicRules in codeA strategy tested statistically and executed by a program. No emotion at entry. Weakness: it requires programming, and the trap is "fitting" to the past.
Mentor's note

Schools are not religions. They are different glasses for the same chart. The danger is wearing six pairs at once: then you will always find a reason for a trade. Pick one main pair of glasses and one for checking.

SMC · Smart Money ConceptsAn approach that reads structure and liquidity through the eyes of the big players.
ICT · Inner Circle TraderMichael Huddleston's methodology, the source of much of the SMC vocabulary.
Overfitting · нагласяванеA strategy tuned so precisely to the past that it does not work in the future.

●●30.3 Order flow: indices versus forex

Order flow is the most honest pair of "glasses" because it looks at real trades. But it works fully only where there is a central exchange that sees all orders.

Index futures (ES, NQ)Spot forex (EURUSD)
Where it tradesOne exchange: CMEHundreds of banks and brokers, no centre
VolumeReal, every contractTick volume: the number of price changes at your broker
Footprint and deltaComplete and accurateApproximate
DOM (order book)VisibleOnly your broker's, not the market's
WorkaroundNot neededThe CME 6E (euro) future as a proxy

That is why order flow traders usually trade indices and futures, while forex traders rely on structure, liquidity and sessions. If you want order flow on the euro, watch 6E and carry the read over to EURUSD.

FootprintA chart showing contracts bought and sold at each price within the candle.
Delta · делтаBought minus sold with market orders over a period.
DOM · Depth of MarketThe book of resting limit orders above and below the price.
Tick volumeThe number of price changes over a period. A volume proxy in spot forex.

●●30.4 ICT in brief

ICT is the most discussed school on YouTube and so deserves a sober look. Here are the main ideas, translated into this book's language.

ConceptWhat it meansWhere it is in the book

Myth

"Price is driven by a hidden algorithm (IPDA) that ICT has uncovered."

Reality

There is no public evidence of such an algorithm. Concepts like liquidity, sessions and FVG describe useful repetitions in price behaviour. Treat them as hypotheses you test with a backtest, not as a law.

OTE · Optimal Trade EntryEntry on a pullback to between 62% and 79% of the last move (by Fibonacci).
PD arrayA price zone the market is likely to return to: OB, FVG, an old high or low.
Power of 3 · AMDAccumulation, manipulation, distribution.

●30.5 Which style is for you

Answer honestly. The result is a starting point, not a verdict. You can change style later, but not before you have done 90 days with one.

The 1-1-1 rule

One style, one market, one session for the first 90 days (Chapter 34). If after that you have 30+ trades with positive expectancy, then you add a second. Not before.

Screen time · време пред екранаThe hours you spend watching charts. A resource the style demands.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 30

  • The horizon (scalp, day, swing, position) sets the timeframe, the stop and the screen time.
  • The smaller the stop, the bigger the share of risk eaten by spread and commission.
  • The school (price action, SMC, ICT, order flow, macro, algorithmic) sets what you read. One main, one for checking.
  • Full order flow exists where there is a central exchange: indices and futures.
  • ICT concepts are useful hypotheses. Test them with a backtest.
  • The 1-1-1 rule: one style, one market, one session for 90 days.

No single right answer

A trader works from 9 to 18 and has 40 free minutes in the morning and an hour in the evening. They watch ICT videos and want to scalp the Silver Bullet at 17:00 Sofia time on M1 with a 4-pip stop. Their broker has a 1.0 spread and 0.7 pips commission.

Task: what are the two problems in the plan? Which style and window fit their life better, and how will they carry over the ideas they like?

Sample answer
  1. Time: at 17:00 they are at work. A plan that needs a screen during working hours will be broken or traded from a phone under pressure.
  2. Costs: 1.7 pips of cost with a 4-pip stop is 0.43R per trade. It takes a huge winning streak just to cover the fees.
  3. Swing on H1–H4 fits better: analysis in the evening, limit orders with a stop and a target, a check in the morning. A 30–60-pip stop brings the cost below 6% of the risk.
  4. The ideas stay: liquidity, FVG and premium/discount work on H4 too. Kill zone logic is replaced by daily and weekly levels. Which style to choose is their decision, but the schedule and the account have already voted.
