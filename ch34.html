Daily and Weekly Routine

We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit. Will Durant, summarising Aristotle

After this chapter you will be able to

  • lay out your week from the Sunday ritual to the Friday review;
  • write a morning brief that says what matters today and when;
  • turn the session plan into "if–then" sentences;
  • run a backtest that resembles reality, not a dream.

●37.1 The shape of the week

Mon–Fri · morningBrief15–20 minutes before London: what happened overnight, what comes out today, changes to the matrix, zones and alerts.
Mon–Fri · sessionExecutionOnly by the "if–then" plan. A trade limit. Alerts instead of watching.

Mentor's note

A routine is not a prison but an autopilot for the boring parts. The less you think about when and how to prepare, the more attention is left for the decisions that really matter.

●●37.2 The morning brief: two annotated examples

A good brief answers six questions: what changed, what comes out today and at what time, what the market expects, what would surprise me, which levels matter and what the verdict for the pair is. Here are two real briefs from the notes, retold and annotated.

What changed
Monday was a US holiday, a thin market. Oil is swinging on ceasefire news. Positioning is crowded long dollar.
Today
US CPI at 15:30 Sofia time. This is the event of the week.
Scenarios
Core CPI 0.3% or more: dollar up, gold down. Below 0.3%: the opposite.
What happened
Core comes in at 0.3%. The dollar jumps, gold falls, EURUSD to a 16-month low. The scenario plays out exactly.
Why the brief is good

One main event, clearly named. The scenario is written as "if number, then direction", not as a forecast. Positioning is noted separately. In the evening there is something to compare against.

What changed
UK CPI comes out at 9:00: 3.5% versus a 3.4% forecast. Oil rises again overnight as hopes of talks with Iran evaporate.
Today
Bailey (BoE) speaks at 16:00, EIA oil data at 17:30, tech company earnings after the US close.
Read
The surprise is only 0.1 pp, core is calm. The pound's first move may reverse. Bailey is the second window.
Risks
News about Iran can move oil, gold and the dollar in seconds. Oil prices differ between sources.
Why the brief is good

It registers not just the number but the size of the surprise: 0.1 pp is small, so there is a warning that the first move may fade. There is a second window (Bailey) that can change the read. There is a note on data quality. Everything has Sofia times.

●●37.3 The "if–then" plan

The brief says what matters. The plan says what you do. Write it as "if–then" sentences. When the situation arrives, you don't think, you read. Chapter 15 showed the idea; here is the tool. The entries are saved in your browser.

Implementation intention · намерение за изпълнениеThe psychological name for the "if–then" plan. Research shows it increases the chance of carrying out the intention.

●37.4 The evening review

  1. The journal. Every trade and every missed setup, with scores and tags.
  2. The scenarios. Which morning scenario came true? Which was wrong? Which did not trigger?
  3. The plan. Did I follow every "if–then"? If not, which one and why?
  4. One sentence. What I learned today that goes into tomorrow's plan.
  5. Done. You close the charts. The market will be there tomorrow.

StudentAnd if I didn't trade at all today?

MentorThen the review matters even more. A day without a trade in which there was no setup is a successful day. A day without a trade in which the setup was there and you hesitated is a lesson. Only the entry tells you which of the two it was.

Scenario scoring · оценка на сценариитеCame true, wrong, did not trigger, not gradable.

●●37.5 A backtest that resembles reality

Backtesting is the fastest way to gain experience. But it is also the easiest way to fool yourself, because you know what happened next. Six rules make it honest.

RuleWhy
Random dates, not your favouritesOtherwise you pick the days when the setup worked.
Hidden right side (Bar Replay in TradingView)You must not see the future while you decide.
You record the missed ones tooOtherwise you count only the pretty examples.
Spread and commission in the resultIn scalping they are part of the stop (Chapter 32).
The day's news from the calendarA setup 5 minutes before CPI is not the same setup.
The same fields as in the journalSo you can compare the backtest with the demo.
Expect a difference

Demo results are usually worse than the backtest, and live results worse than demo. That is not a sign the method doesn't work. It is the price of real time and emotion. If the gap is huge, look for it in the execution tags.

Bar ReplayA TradingView feature that plays history candle by candle.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 37

  • Sunday: ritual. Morning: brief. Session: execution. Evening: review. Friday: weekly review.
  • The brief answers six questions and gives the Sofia times.
  • The plan is in "if–then" sentences, including when you don't enter and when you stop.
  • The scenarios are graded every evening.
  • The honest backtest: random dates, a hidden future, costs, news, the same fields.

No single right answer

Wednesday. UK CPI comes out at 9:00: 3.5% versus a 3.4% forecast, core below expectations. GBPUSD jumps 40 pips. The BoE governor speaks at 16:00. The weekly bias for the pound is "up, ~56%, conviction 3". You trade London from 10:00 to 12:00.

Task: write the brief using the six rows. Then write at least four "if–then" sentences for the session, including one for "I don't enter" and one for the end of the day.

Sample answer
  1. Brief: (1) UK CPI hot by 0.1 pp, core soft; GBP +40 pips. (2) A BoE speaker at 16:00. (3) The market already expects a hike. (4) A soft tone at 16:00 would be a surprise. (5) Levels: the post-CPI high, the Asian low. (6) Yellow: the direction matches the bias, but the surprise is small and there is a second window.
  2. If price comes back into the zone below the post-CPI high and takes the liquidity below it, then I look for a buy by the rules with 1% risk.
  3. If price keeps going up without a pullback, then I don't chase: I log it as "missed by plan".
  4. If price comes back below the pre-CPI level, then the bias for the day is neutral and I don't trade the pound.
  5. If it is 12:00 or I have two losses, then I stop. If I have a position at 15:45, then I close it or I am at breakeven before 16:00. The decisions are yours, but at 10:00 they must already be written down.
