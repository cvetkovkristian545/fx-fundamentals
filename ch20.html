What Moves a Currency

In economics, things take longer to happen than you think they will, and then they happen faster than you thought they could. Rudi Dornbusch, economist, author of the exchange rate overshooting model

After this chapter you will be able to

  • rank a currency's drivers by horizon: days, months, years;
  • explain the rate differential, the carry trade and why carry unwinds suddenly;
  • say why USD, JPY and CHF are safe havens, while AUD, CAD, NZD and NOK are commodity currencies;
  • score a pair with a simple strength table for its two currencies.

●20.1 The exchange rate is a price, and the price has three horizons

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. Like any price, it moves with supply and demand. Who wants euros? Exporters who receive dollars and convert them. Investors buying European bonds. Tourists. Speculators. Central banks building reserves. The question is which of them dominates in a given period.

Mentor's note

An intraday trader lives in the first row, but the wind comes from the second. The third row is the climate. You cannot change it, but it is good to know whether it is summer or winter.

An important note from Sowell: a "strong" currency does not mean a "better" economy. A strong currency makes exports dearer and hurts exporters. A weak one helps exports but makes imports and energy dearer. That is why governments sometimes want a weaker currency, and central banks intervene in both directions.

●●20.2 Rate differential and divergence

The most important medium-term driver is the rate gap between the two currencies in a pair. If US 2-year bonds pay significantly more than German ones, capital has an incentive to be in dollars. When the gap widens in the dollar's favour, EURUSD usually falls. When it narrows, EURUSD usually rises.

Traders watch 2-year yields because they best reflect expectations for the central bank over the next few years. Even better is to watch the real gap, after inflation.

PairSpread to watchIf the spread widens in favour of the first currency

AUDUSDAustralia 2y − US 2yAUDUSD up (but China and risk too)

Beyond today's rate gap, what matters is who starts the cycle first. If the Fed is already hiking while the ECB is still thinking, the divergence favours the dollar, even if rates are still close. The market trades the path, not the point.

Rate differential · лихвен диференциалThe gap in rates or yields between two countries.
Policy divergence · разминаванеTwo central banks going in different directions.

●●20.3 The carry trade and how it unwinds

Carry trade: you borrow in a low-rate currency (historically the Japanese yen and the Swiss franc) and hold a high-rate currency. You earn the difference every day as long as the exchange rate stays put. The problem: the profit takes the stairs up, and the loss takes the lift down.

In August 2024 the world saw the yen carry trade unwind. The Bank of Japan raised rates, US jobs data came out weak, and everyone who had borrowed in yen rushed to close at once. USDJPY fell from about 162 in early July to about 142 on 5 August, and Japan's Nikkei index had its worst day since 1987. Chapter 29 looks at the case in detail.

Unwind signals

A jump in volatility (VIX), the funding currency's central bank turning more hawkish, crowded positioning (COT data), a sharp risk-off. When two of these signals light up together, carry pairs become dangerous.

●20.4 Safe havens and commodity currencies

USD

The world's reserve currency, the most liquid market. Under global stress everyone looks for dollars. The exception: when the stress comes from the US itself.

JPY

Japan is a net creditor. Under stress Japanese investors bring money home and the carry trade unwinds. Both mean buying yen.

CHF

Political stability, net creditor, a strong bank. The SNB sometimes intervenes against a franc that is too strong.

Gold

A safe haven with no political risk. It sometimes rises together with the dollar when the fear is about the system, not about one asset.

On the other side are the commodity currencies, whose strength follows the price of what the country exports:

In risk-off, commodity currencies usually fall and safe havens rise. Chapter 22 shows what this looks like across all markets at once.

Commodity currency · стокова валутаThe currency of a large raw materials exporter.

●●20.5 The long-term anchors: PPP and the balance of payments

Purchasing power parity (PPP): in the long run the exchange rate should equalise the price of the same basket in both countries. The best-known illustration is The Economist's "Big Mac index": if the burger costs 5 dollars in the US and 5.50 euros in the euro area, the "fair" rate would be 0.91 dollars per euro. If the actual rate is 1.10, the euro is overvalued by about 21% on this measure.

The balance of payments records everything that crosses the border: goods, services, income (the current account) and investment (the financial account). A country with a large current account deficit depends on foreign capital to cover it. As long as the capital keeps coming, all is well. When it suddenly stops (a sudden stop), the currency can collapse. That is how the 1997 Asian crisis began (Chapter 27).

Myth

A trade deficit means the country is losing.

Reality

The US has run a trade deficit for decades and is still the richest country. A current account deficit means foreigners are investing in the country. The problem arises when the capital stops coming, not the deficit itself.

PPP · паритет на покупателната способностThe exchange rate at which a basket costs the same in both countries.
Current account · текуща сметкаGoods, services, income and transfers with the rest of the world.
Sudden stop · внезапно спиранеCapital stops flowing into the country.

●20.6 DXY and correlations

DXY measures the dollar against a basket of six currencies with fixed weights: EUR 57.6%, JPY 13.6%, GBP 11.9%, CAD 9.1%, SEK 4.2%, CHF 3.6%. That is why DXY is almost a mirror of EURUSD. Many traders start the day with DXY: first they ask what the dollar is doing, then they pick the pair.

●●●Go deeperCovered interest parity and forward points▾

Why can't you borrow in yen at 0.5%, buy dollars at 4% and lock in the exchange rate for a risk-free profit? Because the forward rate already removes the gap. Covered interest parity: forward = spot × (1 + quote rate) ÷ (1 + base rate). With EURUSD at 1.1000, the dollar rate at 4% and the euro rate at 2%, the one-year forward is about 1.1216: 216 forward points above spot.

That is also where your swap comes from: holding a position overnight is like rolling a one-day forward. The higher-yielding side earns, the lower-yielding side pays, minus the broker's markup.

In calm markets the parity holds almost exactly. In 2008 and March 2020 it broke: borrowing dollars through the FX market became far more expensive than the rates implied. That gap, the cross-currency basis, is one of the clearest signs of dollar funding stress (Appendix E).

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 20

  • The exchange rate is a price with three horizons: days (news), months (rates), years (inflation and balances).
  • The rate differential and which bank leads the cycle are the main medium-term wind.
  • Carry earns slowly and loses fast; it unwinds when volatility jumps.
  • USD, JPY, CHF and gold are safe havens; AUD, CAD, NZD, NOK follow commodities.
  • PPP and the balance of payments are long-term anchors, not entry signals.
  • DXY is almost an inverted EURUSD; check your total USD exposure.

Tuesday morning. The spread between German and US 2-year bonds has narrowed by 15 bp in the euro's favour over the week. The ECB sounds more hawkish than expected. Oil has jumped 8% on geopolitics. VIX is at 28 and rising. You have been long EURUSD since yesterday and are thinking of adding a long AUDUSD.

Task: which drivers favour EURUSD and which are against it? What does the risk environment say for AUDUSD? What is your total exposure if you add? What would the strength table look like?

Sample answer
  1. In favour of EURUSD: the spread is narrowing towards the euro, the ECB is more hawkish. Against: risk-off (VIX 28 and rising) usually helps the dollar; oil hits Europe as an energy importer.
  2. AUDUSD: risk-off is a bad environment for a commodity currency, even if raw material prices are rising. Oil is not Australia's main commodity.
  3. If you add, you hold two short-USD bets in an environment where the dollar is a safe haven. The risk is doubled, and correlation strengthens under stress.
  4. Table: EUR about +1 (rates +1, CB +1, energy −1), USD about +1 (risk +1). The wind is mixed, verdict YELLOW. Sensible: do not add AUDUSD; manage EURUSD by the rules of Chapter 12.
