Risk and Position Size

To succeed, you must first survive. Market saying, often linked to Warren Buffett

After this chapter you will be able to

  • explain why 1% risk per trade is the foundation of survival;
  • calculate how much you need to gain to recover from a 10%, 20% or 50% drawdown;
  • set a daily loss limit and reduce size when the market is stormy.

●11.1 Why exactly 1%

In Chapter 2 you calculated a position size at 1% risk. Now, why that number. The answer is in the arithmetic of losses. If you risk 1% per trade, ten losses in a row (which will happen, Chapter 5) take the account down about 9.6%. If you risk 5%, the same ten losses take it down 40%. If you risk 10%, 65%.

Mentor's note

The market doesn't punish a wrong analysis. It punishes the wrong size. At 1% you can be wrong for a long time and still learn. At 10% you learn only once.

Risk per trade · риск на сделкаThe percentage of the account you lose if the stop is hit.
Risk of ruin · риск от фалитThe probability of losing so much that you can't continue.

●11.2 The arithmetic of drawdown

Drawdown and recovery are not symmetrical. If you lose 50%, you don't need to gain 50% to get back. You need to gain 100%. The deeper the hole, the harder it is to climb out.

Drawdown · спадThe difference between the account's highest value and its current value, in percent.
Max drawdown · максимален спадThe deepest drawdown over a period. Prop firms cap it.

●●11.3 A daily limit and constant risk in money

Besides risk per trade, you need a daily limit: an amount after which the day is over, whatever you see on the chart. The reason isn't mathematical but human: after two losses in a row, decisions usually get worse. The rule in the framework this book comes from is strict: if you lose and feel angry, the day is over. Other experienced traders use a limit of 2–3R a day.

Second rule: risk the same amount, not the same number of lots. If the stop is 10 pips, the position is bigger; if it is 25 pips, smaller. The risk in money stays the same. That way no trade matters more than the others.

Daily loss limit · дневен лимитThe maximum loss for the day, after which you stop.
Fixed fractional · фиксиран процентYou risk the same percentage of the account on every trade.

●●11.4 Sizing by volatility

When the market is stormy (around news, in a geopolitical shock, after a big gap), the usual stop is no longer enough. You have two choices: widen the stop and reduce the size so the risk in money stays the same, or stay out. What you do not do is keep the size and widen the stop. Then your risk quietly grows along with the stop.

Some analysts add a second layer: with a B grade (Chapter 10) or a yellow macro verdict they risk half the usual amount. Risk becomes a function of the quality of the picture.

Myth

This trade is a sure thing, so I'll risk more.

Reality

There is no sure trade. The feeling of certainty is exactly when the risk rule is most often broken, and most often paid for.

●●●Go deeperRisk of ruin and the Kelly fraction▾

The Kelly formula gives the fraction of the account that maximises long-run growth for a known edge: f = W − (1 − W) ÷ R, where W is the win rate and R the average win in R-multiples. With W = 40% and R = 3: f = 0.40 − 0.60 ÷ 3 = 0.20, or 20% per trade.

Nobody sane risks 20%. Three reasons: your real W and R are estimates with large error; the formula assumes you know them exactly; and full Kelly produces drawdowns of 50% and more along the way. Professionals use a fraction of Kelly, and 1% is about one twentieth of it in this example. That margin is the price of not knowing your edge precisely.

The useful lesson: if Kelly for your stats is negative, there is no edge, and no position size makes it positive.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 11

  • 1% per trade: ten losses in a row = about −9.6%. You survive to learn.
  • Drawdown and recovery are not symmetrical: −50% needs +100%.
  • A daily limit in R and the rule “angry = end of the day”.
  • Constant risk in money; size changes with the stop.
  • In high volatility: a wider stop and smaller size, or skip.

No single right answer

Demo account $10,000. This week: Monday −1R, Tuesday −1R and −1R, Wednesday +3R, Thursday −1R. On Friday morning you see an A+ picture, but the stop has to be 40 pips, because there was big news last night and ATR is double. You normally trade 0.6 lots at 15–17 pips.

Task: where are you for the week in R? Are you close to the 5R weekly limit? What size would it be at a 40-pip stop and 1% risk? What is the sensible thing to do?

Sample answer
  1. Week: −1 −1 −1 +3 −1 = −1R. Far from the 5R weekly limit.
  2. Size at 40 pips: $100 ÷ (40 × $10) = 0.25 lots. Not 0.6.
  3. If you trade 0.6 lots with a 40-pip stop, you risk $240, 2.4% of the account. That is the quiet doubling.
  4. Sensible: 0.25 lots, or skip if the wide stop puts the target under 3R. And remember that on Friday afternoon liquidity thins before the weekend.
