Positioning and Sentiment

When everyone thinks alike, no one is thinking. Attributed to Walter Lippmann

After this chapter you will be able to

  • read the COT report and turn a net position into a 0–100 index;
  • use retail sentiment as a contrarian signal, and know when not to;
  • read options: implied volatility, the expected move, risk reversals and large expiries;
  • recognise from price alone that a view is already "in the price".

●21.1 Who is already in

Chapter 20 explains why a currency should move. This chapter asks a different question: who still has to buy? If everyone who likes the dollar has already bought it, good news can no longer push it much higher. But bad news can make all of them sell at once.

Think of a cinema with one exit. While the film is good, nobody moves. The fuller the cinema, the worse the rush when someone shouts "fire". In Appendix E almost every big move had the same fuel: crowded positioning.

Myth

If most people are long, the price must go up.

Reality

A price goes up when new buyers arrive. If most people are already long, the pool of new buyers is small and the pool of potential sellers is large.

Positioning · позициониранеHow market participants are already placed: long, short, how much.
Sentiment · настроениеHow participants feel about a market, measured by surveys, positions or prices.

●●21.2 The COT report, step by step

Every Friday at 15:30 New York time (22:30 Sofia) the US regulator CFTC publishes the Commitments of Traders report. It shows positions in US futures as of the previous Tuesday. Currency futures on the CME are quoted against the dollar: a long position in the euro contract is a bet on a higher EURUSD, a long position in the yen contract is a bet on a lower USDJPY.

Category (TFF report)Who it isHow to read it
Leveraged fundsHedge funds, CTAs, fast moneyTrend followers. Extremes here are the classic "crowded trade".
Asset managersPension and investment fundsSlow money. Their shifts are bigger but take months.
DealersBanks that make marketsUsually the other side of clients. Rarely a signal by themselves.
Non-commercial (legacy report)All large speculators togetherThe number most often quoted in the news.

From a number to an index

A net long of 80,000 contracts says nothing by itself. Is that a lot for this currency? The COT index puts the current net position inside its range over the last three years (156 weeks): 0 means the most short, 100 the most long in that period.

Four limits of COT

1) Futures are a small part of FX: most trading is over the counter, between banks. 2) The data is three days old when it comes out. 3) An extreme can last for months while the trend continues: an extreme is a condition, not a trigger. 4) Changes week to week are noisy. The level and the direction over several weeks matter more.

CFTCCommodity Futures Trading Commission: the US regulator of futures markets.
TFF · Traders in Financial FuturesThe COT version that splits positions into dealers, asset managers, leveraged funds and others.
Net position · нетна позицияLongs minus shorts.
COT index · COT индексWhere the net position sits in its range over the last three years, from 0 to 100.

●●21.3 Retail sentiment: the crowd at the broker

Many brokers publish the share of their clients who are long and short each pair. It is the only positioning data that comes out in real time, and it has one well-known habit: the retail crowd tends to be on the wrong side of trends.

  1. It buys falling prices because they look "cheap", and sells rising ones because they look "expensive".
  2. It takes profits fast and holds losses long, so in a trend it ends up holding the losing side.
  3. Its stops sit at obvious places, which is exactly the liquidity from Chapter 7.

70%+ long and price fallingThe trend down has fuel: these longs are future sellersNot a timing tool

●●●21.4 What options tell you

You do not need to trade options to read them. Option prices show what professionals are willing to pay for protection, which is more honest than what they say.

Implied volatility and the expected move

Implied volatility (IV) is the yearly move the option price "expects", in percent. From it you can estimate a one-standard-deviation move for any period: price × IV × √(days ÷ 365). Before big events overnight IV jumps: the market prices the event in advance.

Risk reversals: which way is fear?

A 25-delta risk reversal compares the price of protection on the upside (calls) with protection on the downside (puts). Negative EURUSD risk reversals mean people pay more to protect against a fall in the euro. When risk reversals hit an extreme, the fear is crowded too.

Large expiries

Options expire at fixed times, most often at the "New York cut", 10:00 New York (17:00 Sofia). A very large expiry near the current price can act like a magnet in the hours before the cut, as banks hedge their options. After 17:00 that pull disappears. Many news sites list the day's large expiries in the morning.

For a scalper

If price keeps returning to a round number like 1.1000 all morning and a large expiry sits there, do not expect a clean breakout before 17:00. After the cut the range often breaks.

Implied volatility · подразбираща се волатилностThe volatility built into option prices: what the market expects.
Risk reversal · риск реверсалThe difference between the volatility of calls and puts with the same delta: shows which way the fear is.
NY cut · нюйоркски срокThe usual expiry time of FX options: 10:00 New York, 17:00 Sofia.

●●21.5 Price as a sentiment gauge

The most up-to-date positioning data is price itself. Two questions after every important piece of news:

Good news, no rise

The news was already in the price. Buyers are tired. The risk is on the downside.

Bad news, no fall

Sellers are exhausted. Shorts may soon have to cover.

A break that fails fast

Stops above or below were taken, but no new money followed. Chapter 7's sweep.

The pain trade

The move that hurts most people at that moment. It is often the one that happens next.

StudentSo I should always trade against the crowd?

MentorNo. In a trend the crowd is wrong for a long time and you would lose for a long time with it. Positioning tells you where the fuel is. Price and your setup tell you when it catches fire.

Pain trade · болезнената сделкаThe move that would hurt the largest number of positioned traders.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 21

  • Positioning is the fuel. The news is the match.
  • COT index: where the net position sits in its 3-year range. An extreme is a condition, not a trigger.
  • The retail crowd is usually wrong in trends. Use it at extremes, never alone.
  • Expected move = price × IV × √(days ÷ 365).
  • Good news with no rise means the news is in the price.

No single right answer

Your macro view on EURUSD is bearish: the Fed is more hawkish than the ECB. The COT index for EUR is 8. Retail traders are 74% short EURUSD. Overnight implied volatility is unusually high because US CPI comes out tomorrow. Price fell for three weeks but did not make a new low on yesterday's bad euro data.

Task: what does positioning say about your bearish view? Which piece of evidence matters most? How would you describe the risk around tomorrow's CPI?

Sample answer
  1. The bearish view is right on macro but crowded: speculators are near their most short in three years and the retail crowd is short as well. Few sellers are left.
  2. The strongest evidence is price: bad euro news and no new low. Sellers are tired.
  3. The pain trade is up. A soft CPI could trigger a squeeze. A hot CPI may give only a small fall, because it is expected. The risk is asymmetric against new shorts. Whether to trade it is my decision; the reading favours caution.
