Prop Firms Compared

Don't focus on making money; focus on protecting what you have. Paul Tudor Jones (attributed)

After this chapter you will be able to

  • explain how a prop challenge works: phases, target, daily and maximum loss, minimum days;
  • tell a static limit from a trailing one and see when the second throws you out earlier;
  • compare several firms by price and rules, with the date of the check;
  • calculate a rough chance of passing a challenge with your own numbers.
Prices with a date

The data in this chapter were checked on 21 October 2026 on the official FTMO website and in public comparisons updated between 10 and 16 October 2026. Prop firms change prices, promotions and rules almost every month. Before buying, read all the rules on the official site, not just the table.

●33.1 What a prop firm really is

A prop firm (from proprietary trading) lets you trade an account with large money if you first pass a challenge. You pay a fee for the challenge. If you pass and then make a profit, you get a large share of it, usually 80–90%.

The honest truth the ads do not put on the front page: at most firms for retail traders both the challenge and the "funded" account are simulated. You trade on a demo, and the firm pays you a share of the simulated profit from its own revenue, much of which is the fees of people who don't pass. That does not make the model a scam; it makes it a business in which most customers lose the fee.

Plus

You risk only the fee, not a large sum. You learn to follow strict risk rules.

Minus

Many people lose the fee again and again. The rules punish mistakes that would slide on a personal account.

The firm's risk

In 2023–2024 several firms shut down or withheld payouts. Choose firms with a long history and public payouts.

Who it is for

For someone with a proven process on demo (Chapter 34), not for someone who "wants to give it a try".

●●33.2 The challenge rules

RuleExample (FTMO 2-Step)What it means
Profit target10% in phase 1, 5% in phase 2You have to reach this profit.
Maximum daily loss5%You cannot lose more than this in one day. Open positions count too.
Maximum loss10%, staticThe account can never fall below 90% of the start.
Minimum trading days4 per phaseYou cannot pass with one big trade.
Time limitNo time limitNo pressure to hurry. Some firms do have a time limit.
Consistency ruleNone on 2-Step; on 1-Step the best day ≤ 50% of the profitThe whole profit cannot come from one day.

The most important difference between firms is how the maximum loss is calculated. A static one stays in one place. A trailing one rises with your highest balance, usually at the end of the day. Try it: first take the account up, then bring it down.

Static drawdown · статичен лимитCalculated from the starting balance and does not move.
Trailing drawdown · плъзгащ се лимитFollows the highest balance or equity. Profit "raises the floor".
EOD · край на деняWith EOD trailing the limit moves only with the end-of-day balance.

How to read a table like this

Don't look at the price first. Look at the daily loss relative to your risk. With 1% risk and a 3% daily limit, three losses in a row in one day end the challenge. With a 5% limit you have five. A scalper with several trades a day has to think about exactly this.

●●●33.4 The maths of the challenge

Enter your numbers. The calculator plays the challenge 2,000 times with random sequences of wins and losses and counts how many times you reach the target before hitting a limit.

StudentSo with 2% risk I'll pass faster?

MentorYou'll reach either the target or the limit faster. Try it in the calculator. Most people find that with double the risk the chance falls rather than rises. The challenge does not reward courage, it rewards survival.

Expectancy · очакванеThe average profit per trade in R: win × probability − loss × probability.
Risk of ruin · риск от разорениеThe probability of hitting the loss limit before the target.

FTMO offers a free trial: 14 days, with half the target (5%) and the same loss limits. You can do an unlimited number of trials, one at a time. It ends if you don't trade in the first 7 days. Passing it does not give a funded account, but it is the cheapest way to see how you react to the rules.

Mentor's note

If your budget is about 75 euros a month, one 10K challenge at FTMO is more than a month's budget. That is not bad. It means every attempt has to be earned: at least two consecutive free trials passed with the process respected before you pay. The fee is not a lottery ticket. It is an exam fee.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 33

  • Most prop accounts are simulated. The firm pays a share of the simulated profit.
  • Target, daily loss, maximum loss, minimum days, time limit, consistency.
  • A trailing limit eats the profit as a buffer; a static one does not.
  • Compare firms first by the daily limit relative to your risk, then by price.
  • Bigger risk rarely increases the chance of passing.
  • The free trial is a mandatory test before a paid challenge.

No single right answer

A scalper has been on demo for two months: 46 trades, 39% winners, average win 3.1R, 1% risk, usually 2–3 trades a day. For two weeks in that period they broke their own rule on the number of trades. They are torn between FTMO 2-Step 10K (5% daily limit, static 10%) and 1-Step (3% daily, 10% EOD trailing), because the second is cheaper.

Task: which format fits their style and why? What does the calculator say with their numbers? Are they ready at all, and what would they check first?

Sample answer
  1. With 2–3 trades a day and 1% risk, a 3% daily limit is reached with three losses, and with 39% winners three losses in a row in one day are not rare. A 5% limit gives more breathing room. The 1-Step trailing also shrinks the buffer after the first profits. The cheaper format is probably the more expensive one for this style.
  2. With 39% and 3.1R the expectancy is about +0.6R per trade, which is good. The calculator with 2-Step rules gives a high chance, but with 1-Step rules (set the daily limit to 3%) the chance drops visibly. The number is a guide, because 46 trades is a small sample.
  3. 46 trades is above the threshold of 30, but the two weeks of broken rules are a more important signal than the win rate. First: another two to four weeks of demo without a violation, then a free trial. The decision is theirs, but the data say the process is not stable yet.
