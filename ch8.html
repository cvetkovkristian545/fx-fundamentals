Top-Down Analysis

It never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting. Jesse Livermore, in “Reminiscences of a Stock Operator” (Edwin Lefèvre, 1923)

After this chapter you will be able to

  • move from D1 to M5 in the right order and say what question you ask each timeframe;
  • score the day's direction with points and record it in a template;
  • decide GO, WAIT or SKIP when the timeframes contradict each other.

●8.1 The four questions

Top-down analysis is like navigation: first you look at the map of the country, then the city, then the street and finally the building number. If you start with the number, you will reach the right building in the wrong city.

TimeframeRoleQuestion
D1 (and W1)DirectionBullish or bearish? Are we in premium or discount? Where is the next big liquidity?
H4SwingDoes it confirm D1? Which way was the last BOS?
H1SetupIs there a POI in the right half? Is there liquidity before it?
M15 / M5DetailHas liquidity been taken? Is there an MSS? Is price returning into the zone?
Mentor's note

The rule is simple: the smaller timeframe serves the bigger one, never the other way round. M5 can't convince you that D1 is wrong.

HTF · по-висок таймфреймHigher timeframe: D1, H4. The direction.
LTF · по-нисък таймфреймLower timeframe: M15, M5. The detail.
Bias · пристрастие, посокаThe expected direction for the day or the week.

●8.2 When the timeframes agree and when they argue

The best picture is when D1 and H4 point the same way and H1 has just finished a pullback against them. Then the H1 pullback is an opportunity, not a threat. When D1 and H4 argue, the rule is: you wait until H4 lines up (a new HL and a BOS in the D1 direction). A trade against D1 and H4 is skipped.

D1H4H1Decision
BullishBullishPullback down to a POIGO: look for a reaction in the zone
BullishBearishAnyWAIT: wait for H4 to make an HL and a BOS up
BullishBullishStrongly up, in premiumWAIT: don't chase, wait for a pullback
Bullish—Setup downSKIP: H1 against D1
RangeRangeIn the middleSKIP: no edge

Opening levels. Besides highs and lows, many analysts mark the opening price of the week, the month and the quarter. When price is above the weekly open, buyers hold the week; below it, sellers. It is a quick, honest thermometer.

Alignment · съгласуваностWhen several timeframes point the same way.
Weekly open · седмично отварянеThe price at the start of the week. A reference point for the weekly direction.

●●8.3 Scoring the direction and the daily template

So that the direction isn't a “feeling”, you score it with points. Tick what applies to EURUSD D1 today. Then fill in the template: it is the same one you will use every morning (Chapter 37).

GOThe conditions line up; it makes sense to watch for a decision.
WAITThe picture isn't ready; you wait for a specific event.
SKIPNo edge in this market today.

●●8.4 The morning scan: the dollar first

Almost all majors include the dollar. That is why the morning review starts with DXY, the dollar index. If DXY is bearish on D1 and making a pullback up, EURUSD and GBPUSD are probably making a pullback down, and so is gold. You look at DXY first and then find the pair with the cleanest picture.

  1. DXY on D1 and H4. What direction is the dollar going?
  2. Currency strength. Compare EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD: which currency is strongest and which weakest this week? The cleanest moves are between the strongest and the weakest.
  3. Multi-asset view. Gold, the S&P 500, the Nasdaq, bitcoin: a mood for risk or for flight (Chapter 22)?
  4. Choice. One, at most two pairs for the day. You only watch the rest.
Myth

The more pairs I follow, the more opportunities I have.

Reality

The more pairs you follow, the more mediocre setups you will find. Two pairs you know well give better decisions than ten.

One more patience rule. When price has broken out strongly without a pullback, there are only two sensible ways to take part: wait for a return to the zone or wait for a second break that confirms the first. Chasing in the middle of the move is the beginner's most expensive habit.

Currency strength · сила на валутатаA comparison of which currency is gaining and which losing against all the others.
Watchlist · списък за наблюдениеThe small set of markets you follow every day.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 08

  • D1 direction → H4 swing → H1 setup → M15/M5 detail. The small serves the big.
  • D1 + H4 in one direction and an H1 pullback = the best picture. Conflict = you wait.
  • The direction score runs from 0 to 8 and is recorded in a template with GO / WAIT / SKIP.
  • The scan starts with DXY and picks one or two pairs.
  • Don't chase: wait for a pullback or a second break.

No single right answer

Monday morning. DXY on D1: bearish, but for the last two days it has been rising towards a supply zone. EURUSD on D1: bullish, in discount. EURUSD H4: the last BOS is down (the pullback). H1: price is above the weekly open, Asia is a tight range. FOMC is on Wednesday.

Task: fill in the daily template in your head: score, H4, POI, decision. What has to happen for the decision to move from WAIT to GO?

Sample answer
  1. D1 bullish (+2), in discount (+2). If there is a D1 OB below price, +2. About 4–6 out of 8.
  2. H4 doesn't confirm: the last BOS is down. By the rule that is WAIT, not GO.
  3. DXY is rising into a supply zone: if it reacts down there, EURUSD will probably finish its pullback.
  4. Condition for GO: H4 forms an HL and price closes above the last LH (a BOS up), ideally after a sweep of the Asian or London low liquidity.
  5. FOMC on Wednesday: whatever lines up, new positions stop before the Fed decision (Chapter 13).
