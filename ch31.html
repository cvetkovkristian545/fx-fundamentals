The First 90 Days

Amateurs practise until they get it right. Professionals practise until they can't get it wrong. A saying from sport

After this chapter you will be able to

  • plan the first 12 weeks: reading and backtesting, demo with a journal, a decision;
  • track your progress week by week;
  • judge with numbers, not with feelings, whether you are ready for the next step;
  • choose between more demo, a free trial, a small live account or a paid challenge.

●34.1 Three stages of four weeks

Ninety days sounds like a lot when you want to make money from tomorrow. It sounds like little when you see how much there is in this book. The plan is simple: first you learn and practise without pressure, then you trade on demo as if the money were real, and finally you decide with data.

Weeks 1–4Reading and backtestingParts I and II, one setup, 50 examples from chart history. No trades, not even on demo. The aim is for your eye to recognise the setup without hesitation.
Weeks 5–8Demo with a full journalA realistic balance, 1% risk, every trade and every missed setup in the journal. A Sunday bias file. An evening review.
Weeks 9–12More demo and a decisionYou continue the demo, collect at least 30 trades and at the end of week 12 decide by the criteria in 34.3.
Mentor's note

Weeks 1–4 are the most boring and the most important. Whoever skips them later pays the market for the same lessons, with interest. The temptation to "try just one trade" is exactly the muscle you are training.

●●34.3 Are you ready: the criteria

At the end of week 12 fill in the numbers from the journal. Don't round in your own favour.

OutcomeWhenWhy
Another 4 weeks of demoOne of the process criteria is missing (rules, execution, violations)The problem is in the process. Money won't fix it.

Myth

"Demo isn't the same, so the results don't count."

Reality

Half true. The emotion is different, which is why live results are usually worse, not better. If the process doesn't work on demo, it won't suddenly work live.

Sample size · размер на извадкатаHow many trades stand behind a conclusion. Under 20 means almost nothing.
Drawdown · спадThe fall from the account's highest point to the lowest point after it.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 34

  • Weeks 1–4: reading and backtesting, no trades.
  • Weeks 5–12: demo with a realistic balance, 1% risk and a full journal.
  • The decision is made by numbers: trades, rules followed, expectancy, drawdown, execution, weeks without a violation.
  • A process problem is cured with more demo, not with more money.
  • The free trial is the bridge between demo and the paid challenge.

No single right answer

The end of week 12. 34 demo trades, expectancy +0.45R, drawdown 4%, execution score 6.5/10, rules followed 85%. Most violations are the same: entering before confirmation because "price is leaving without me", and a stop placed closer than the rule so the position is bigger. The last two weeks have had no violations.

Task: what do the criteria say? What is the real problem behind the numbers? What would you do over the next four weeks, and how will you know it is fixed?

Sample answer
  1. Trades, expectancy and drawdown are fine. Rules followed (85%), execution (6.5) and weeks without a violation (2) are not. The outcome is another four weeks of demo: the problem is in the process.
  2. The two violations are the same thing: fear of missing the move. The early entry and the tightened stop are attempts to "catch up" with price. It is an execution problem, not a strategy problem, because the expectancy is positive despite them.
  3. One rule for each violation, written as "if–then": "If there is no closed confirmation candle, I don't enter, even if price takes off. A missed trade is logged as missed, not as a loss." And: "The stop goes in first, the size is calculated from it, never the other way round."
  4. Measuring: rules followed above 90%, execution 7 or more, and the number of missed setups logged calmly. If after four weeks the criteria are met, the next step is a free trial. The decision remains theirs, but the numbers will tell them when.
