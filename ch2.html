The Mechanics of a Trade

Rule number one: never lose money. Rule number two: never forget rule number one. Warren Buffett

After this chapter you will be able to

  • work out what one pip is worth at different position sizes;
  • say what a trade really costs: spread, commission, swap;
  • calculate a position size at which you risk exactly 1% of the account.

●2.1 Pip, pipette and lot

Currency prices move in very small steps. The standard unit of measure is the pip. On most pairs the pip is the fourth decimal place: from 1.0850 to 1.0851 is one pip. On yen pairs the pip is the second decimal: from 150.20 to 150.21 is one pip. The fifth decimal (or the third on yen) is a pipette, one tenth of a pip. Platforms show it, but in analysis we count pips.

The quantity you trade is measured in lots:

●2.2 What a trade costs

Every trade has costs before the market has even moved. There are three kinds:

  1. Spread. The difference between bid and ask (Chapter 0.2). You pay it on every entry. On EURUSD in active hours it is usually under one pip, but around news and in the quiet night hours it widens.
  2. Commission. Some accounts (often called RAW or ECN) have a near-zero spread but charge a fixed commission, for example 3 to 3.5 dollars per lot per side. Other accounts (Standard) have no commission but a wider spread.
  3. Swap. If you hold a position past the end of the trading day (around midnight Sofia time), you pay or receive the interest-rate difference between the two currencies. On the night into Thursday a triple swap is usually charged to cover the weekend.

The default numbers are examples for EURUSD. Real ones depend on the broker and on current rates.

Mentor's note

A scalper who makes 20 trades a day pays the spread 20 times. So more trades don't mean more profit. Often they mean a bigger bill for the broker.

Commission · комисионнаA fixed fee per lot that the broker charges separately from the spread.
Swap · суапThe overnight interest difference on an open position. It can be plus or minus.
Round turn · пълен цикълEntry plus exit. Commissions are often quoted “per side”, and a round turn is double that.

●2.3 Leverage and margin

One standard lot of EURUSD is 100,000 euros, i.e. over a hundred thousand dollars. Most people don't have that much. Leverage lets you open a position much bigger than your account. The broker locks only a small part of the value as collateral. That collateral is called margin.

In the EU the regulator ESMA caps leverage for retail clients: up to 30:1 on major currency pairs, up to 20:1 on other currency pairs, gold and major indices, up to 2:1 on cryptocurrencies. Leverage of 30:1 means a 30,000-dollar position needs 1,000 dollars of margin.

Important

Leverage does not increase your chance of winning. It only increases how fast you win or lose. A position 30 times bigger than the account loses 1% of the account on a move of 1/30 of a percent, i.e. about 3–4 pips on EURUSD.

What happens if the position loses? The broker watches the margin level: equity divided by the margin in use. If it falls below a set threshold you get a margin call, a warning. In the EU, if it falls to 50% the broker must start closing your positions. That is a stop out. Drag the move and see when it happens.

Leverage · ливъриджThe ratio between position size and the collateral locked.
Margin · маржинThe amount the broker locks as collateral for an open position.
Equity · собствен капиталBalance plus or minus unrealised profit or loss.

●2.4 Order types

An order is an instruction to the broker. There are a few basic types. Press one to see where it sits relative to the current price.

Myth

My stop loss is guaranteed: I'll exit exactly at that price.

Reality

A stop becomes a market order once price reaches it. In a fast move or at the open after a weekend the fill can be worse. This is called slippage. Some brokers offer a guaranteed stop for an extra fee.

Market order · пазарна поръчкаBuy or sell immediately at the best available price.
Limit order · лимитна поръчкаBuy lower or sell higher than the current price.
Stop order · стоп поръчкаBuy higher or sell lower when price reaches a level.
SL / TPStop loss: exit at a loss. Take profit: exit at a profit.
Slippage · приплъзванеThe difference between the requested and the actual fill price.

●●2.5 Exercise: a position with 1% risk

Here is the most important calculation in the book. You will see it again in Chapter 11. The idea is simple: first you decide how much you can lose, then where the stop is, and only at the end how big the position is. Position size is a result, not a choice.

Lots = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop in pips × Pip value for 1 lot)

StudentWhy not just 1 lot? It looks like a nice round number.

MentorBecause 1 lot with a 15-pip stop risks 150 dollars. On a 1,000-dollar account that is 15%. Seven such losses in a row, and they happen, and the account is cut in half. The market doesn't care about your round numbers.

Risk per trade · риск на сделкаWhat percentage of the account you lose if the stop is hit.
Position sizing · размер на позициятаWorking out the lots from the risk and the stop.
●●●Go deeperWhere your order really goes▾

When you press Buy, your broker does one of two things. In the B-book model it takes the other side itself: your loss is its profit. In the A-book (STP/ECN) model it passes the order on to banks and liquidity providers and earns from the spread or a commission. Most retail brokers mix the two: small or losing accounts stay in-house, consistently profitable ones are passed on.

Liquidity providers may use last look: a few milliseconds to reject a quote if the price has moved. That is one source of requotes and slippage in fast markets.

Rollover happens at 17:00 New York time (00:00 Sofia). In the minutes around it, banks reset their books and spreads often widen sharply. A tight stop or a limit order near price can be filled on that spike alone. On Wednesday night the swap is charged three times, because spot trades settle two days later and the weekend is added.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 02

  • A pip is 0.0001 (0.01 on JPY). 1 lot = 100,000 units; on EURUSD 1 pip per lot = 10 dollars.
  • Costs are spread, commission and swap. More trades = more costs.
  • Leverage increases speed, not probability. In the EU up to 30:1 on majors; stop out at 50%.
  • Limit orders: a better price than now. Stop orders: a worse price, on confirmation.
  • Position size is calculated from risk and stop, never the other way round.

No single right answer

Account of 5,000 dollars. You want to buy EURUSD at 1.0850. The logical place for the stop is below the last low at 1.0828. The target is at 1.0916. On Monday morning you see that the position will be open for at least two nights and that the swap for long EURUSD is negative.

Task: calculate the position size at 1% risk, the reward-to-risk ratio and the approximate cost of spread (0.8 pips) and swap (−6 dollars per lot per night). Is the trade worth it on costs?

Sample answer
  1. Stop: 1.0850 − 1.0828 = 22 pips. Risk: 1% of 5,000 = 50 dollars.
  2. Lots = 50 ÷ (22 × 10) = 0.227, rounded down to 0.22 lots. Actual risk: 22 × 0.22 × 10 = 48.40 dollars.
  3. Target: 1.0916 − 1.0850 = 66 pips. Reward to risk: 66 ÷ 22 = 3R.
  4. Spread: 0.8 × 0.22 × 10 = 1.76 dollars. Swap: 2 nights × 0.22 × −6 = −2.64 dollars. About 4.40 dollars in total, under 0.1R.
  5. The costs are small relative to the risk and the target. The decision depends on the analysis, not on the costs. If the nights include Wednesday, the swap is triple.
