Candles and Charts

There is nothing new on Wall Street. There can't be, because speculation is as old as the hills. Jesse Livermore, in “Reminiscences of a Stock Operator” (Edwin Lefèvre, 1923)

After this chapter you will be able to

  • read every candle: where price started, how far it got and where it closed;
  • say who won the period, buyers or sellers, and how convincingly;
  • understand how 12 small M5 candles become one H1 candle and why that matters.

●3.1 Anatomy of a candle

A chart is not a drawing. It is a record. Every candle records four prices for one period of time: where price started (Open), the highest point (High), the lowest (Low) and where it finished (Close). Traders call them OHLC for short.

Our running example for the whole book is EURUSD, the euro against the dollar. We will come back to it chapter after chapter so you can see how each new idea layers onto a familiar market.

A green (bullish) candle closes above the open: buyers pushed price higher. A red (bearish) candle closes below the open. The colour tells you the direction. The shape tells you how hard it was won.

Candle · свещA graphic of the four prices (OHLC) for one period. Japanese candlesticks, from the 18th century rice trade.
Body · тялоThe distance between Open and Close. It shows the net result of the period.
Wick · фитилAlso called a shadow. It shows prices the market reached but didn't hold.

●3.2 Who won the candle?

Behind every candle there are two sides. Buyers want a higher price and push it up. Sellers want to sell and press it down. The candle is the result of that battle for the period.

A long body and short wicks mean one side dominated from start to finish. A long wick means someone tried but was pushed back. A small body between two wicks means nobody won.

Mentor's note

A candle's colour is its loudest and least important part. Look at where it closed relative to its whole range. That is the real answer.

Bullish · бичиRising. Buyers are in control. The bull strikes upward with its horns.
Bearish · мечиFalling. Sellers are in control. The bear strikes downward with its paw.
Range · диапазонHigh minus Low. On EURUSD it is measured in pips: 0.0001 = 1 pip.

●●3.3 Five candles you will meet every day

The names sound exotic, but each of these candles simply describes a ratio between body and wicks. Learn what they say, not what they are called.

Myth

“I saw a pin bar, so the market will reverse.”

Reality

A candle on its own means almost nothing. A pin bar in the middle of a range is noise. A pin bar after liquidity has been taken, in a zone of interest, in the direction of the higher timeframe, is information. Location decides.

StudentWhy are you so fixated on wicks?

MentorBecause the wick is where the market tried and was refused. The body tells you what happened. The wick tells you what was not allowed to happen.

Engulfing · поглъщанеA body that completely covers the previous candle's body.
Doji · доджиOpen ≈ Close. A draw between buyers and sellers.

●●3.4 Timeframes: one candle is a whole film

One H1 (one-hour) candle contains twelve M5 (five-minute) candles. The H1 candle shows only the start, the end and the two extremes. Everything in between is hidden inside it.

That is why analysts work top-down: D1 gives the direction, H4 and H1 show the picture, and M5 shows the detail. We will build this system in Chapter 8.

Watch on Forex Factory

Candles around red-folder news (NFP, CPI, a Fed decision) are not read like ordinary ones. The first five-minute window after 15:30 Sofia time can have a range as big as a whole quiet hour. Don't read the news candle's wick as a rejection before price has found a new structure. The protocol is in Chapter 13.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 03

  • Every candle records four prices: Open, High, Low, Close.
  • The body shows the net result. The wicks show the rejected prices.
  • Where a candle closed within its range says more than its colour.
  • Engulfing, pin bar, doji, inside bar and marubozu are ratios, not magic. Location decides whether they mean anything.
  • The higher timeframe contains the lower one. Analysis goes top-down.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

EURUSD, H1. The last three candles: a big green candle with small wicks; then a candle with a small body and a long upper wick that goes above the previous high and comes back; after it a red candle whose body fully covers the green one's body. US CPI (red folder) is in 40 minutes. The daily chart is in a downtrend.

Task: describe what you see candle by candle, what it might mean and what you will watch over the next two hours. Write scenario A and B and what would invalidate each.

Sample answer
  1. Candle 1: buyers dominate the whole hour.
  2. Candle 2: price breaks above the high but closes back below. The upper wick is a rejection; the stops above the high were probably taken (liquidity, Chapter 7).
  3. Candle 3: bearish engulfing. Sellers take control and erase the buyers' whole hour.
  4. Context: D1 is falling, so candles 2–3 are in the direction of the higher timeframe. That adds to their weight.
  5. Scenario A (more likely): continuation down after CPI if price stays below candle 3's body. Invalidation: an H1 close above candle 2's wick.
  6. Scenario B: CPI surprises in the euro's favour, price climbs back above the high and closes there. Then the rejection was a trap and the picture flips.
  7. Action before CPI: none. Watch the reaction 15–30 minutes after the number, then see which side kept control.
