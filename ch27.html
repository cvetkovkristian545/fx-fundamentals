Case Files

History doesn't repeat itself, but it often rhymes. Attributed to Mark Twain

After this chapter you will be able to

  • tell five real market events using the same scheme;
  • recognise what was visible before the headline in each of them;
  • calculate how much a gap that jumps your stop can cost you;
  • tell the market's first reaction apart from its digestion of the news.

●29.1 How to read a dossier

Each case in this chapter is told in six steps. Use the same scheme for the events you live through yourself. In a year you will have your own archive, and it is worth more than any book, this one included.

  1. Background: what the picture looks like before the event.
  2. The headline: what exactly happens and when.
  3. What price did: numbers, direction, speed.
  4. Why: the mechanism, not just "the market got scared".
  5. Visible beforehand: what a careful analyst could have seen.
  6. Lesson: one sentence that goes into your rules.

●●29.2 The five dossiers

The Swiss National Bank removes the EURCHF floor

Background
From September 2011 the SNB promises that EURCHF will not fall below 1.20. To hold it, it prints francs and buys euros, for years. Its balance sheet grows to a size comparable with the whole Swiss economy. The market expects the ECB to launch large-scale QE in late January 2015, which will make the defence even more expensive.
The headline
15 January 2015, 10:30 Swiss time (11:30 in Sofia): the SNB removes the floor and cuts the rate to −0.75%. Three days earlier one of its officials had called the floor a "cornerstone" of policy.
What price did
EURCHF falls from 1.20 to about 0.85 within minutes, then comes back to around 1.04 by the end of the day. USDCHF falls from about 1.02 to about 0.74 at the lowest point. Between many quotes there are simply no trades.
Why
Years of accumulated positions rely on the promise. When it vanishes, everyone exits at once, and there are no buyers of euros. Stops fill at the first available price, sometimes hundreds of pips away.
Consequences
The broker FXCM loses about $225 m from client negative balances and needs a rescue loan. Alpari UK goes bust. Traders owe money to their brokers.
Visible beforehand
The SNB's huge balance sheet, the expected ECB QE, the fact that the floor is a promise and not a market price. No one could know the date. One could know that the risk was asymmetric: a little up, a lot down.
Lesson
A stop is not a guarantee. A central bank's promise lasts as long as it suits the bank. Only work with a broker that has negative balance protection.

The British bond crisis and the mini-budget

Background
September 2022. Inflation about 10%. On 22 September the BoE raises the rate by 0.5 pp to 2.25%. A new prime minister, Liz Truss, with a tax-cut programme.
The headline
23 September: the mini-budget promises about £45 bn of tax cuts without an independent costing.
What price did
GBPUSD falls from about 1.13 to about 1.085 the same day. On Monday, 26 September, in the Asian session it reaches about 1.035, a record low. The 30y gilt yield jumps by about 1.3 pp in three days.
Why
The market sees more debt on top of already high inflation and stops trusting the public finances. Pension funds with LDI strategies get margin calls and are forced to sell gilts, which pushes yields up further. Currency and bonds fall together (Chapter 27.4).
Resolution
On 28 September the BoE announces temporary purchases of long gilts. From early October the government withdraws the measures one by one: the chancellor is replaced, and the new one reverses almost everything. Truss resigns on 20 October. GBPUSD is above 1.15 by the end of the month.
Visible beforehand
A weak pound since the summer, rising yields, high inflation, the government's refusal to submit the budget for independent costing.
Lesson
When yields rise and the currency falls together, do not look for "a good rate". Look for who is losing trust.

The unwinding of yen carry trades

Background
For years the BoJ keeps rates around zero while the Fed is above 5%. The world borrows in yen and buys everything with a higher yield. In early July 2024 USDJPY is around 161.9, the highest since 1986. Speculative yen shorts are the largest in about 17 years.
The headline
Not one but a chain: an intervention by Japan's Ministry of Finance (11–12 July), a BoJ hike to 0.25% (31 July), a weak US NFP (2 August, unemployment 4.3%).
What price did
USDJPY falls to about 141.7 on Monday, 5 August, about 20 big figures in a month. The Nikkei loses about 12% in a day, its worst day since 1987. VIX briefly passes 60.
Why
A carry trade pays while the rate gap is large and the currency is calm. When the gap starts to narrow and volatility rises, everyone closes at once: they buy yen to repay the loans. The forced selling spills into stocks around the world.
Resolution
On 7 August the BoJ deputy governor says the bank will not hike while markets are unstable. The panic stops, part of the move comes back.
Visible beforehand
Record positioning (COT), USDJPY at a multi-year high, a central bank just starting to tighten, and US data weakening.
Lesson
A crowded trade does not fall because of the news. It falls because of positioning. The news is only the match.

Bulgaria adopts the euro

Background
From 1 July 1997 the lev is tied by a currency board: first to the German mark, then to the euro at 1.95583. In July 2020 Bulgaria joins ERM II, and in July 2025 the EU approves its entry into the euro area.
The headline
1 January 2026: the euro becomes the official currency. The conversion rate is the same as the board's, 1.95583. In January the lev and the euro are used side by side.
What price did
Nothing. The rate had been fixed for 28 years and was known in advance. The move shows up elsewhere: in the spread of Bulgarian bonds over German ones, which per the notes is about 0.67 pp, and in the credit ratings, upgraded along the road to the euro.
Why
The market prices surprises. An event announced years in advance, at a fixed rate, has nothing to surprise anyone with. The risk "will Bulgaria leave the board" disappears, and that is why the risk premium on the debt falls.
What changes for the trader
Accounts and payments are now in euros, with no conversion from leva. Loan and deposit rates follow the ECB, not the BNB. ECB decisions are now local news as well. For currency trading: there is no pair with the lev, and EUR is your home currency.
Lesson
A big event does not mean a big move. Price moves when reality diverges from expectation.

The hot US CPI on 14 October 2026

Background
Per the notes: the Fed raised to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, oil is high because of the crisis around the Strait of Hormuz. Retail traders are long gold (~68%), the pound (~71%) and the euro (~66%). The scenario in the file: if core CPI is 0.3% or more, the dollar rises and gold falls.
The headline
15:30 Sofia time: CPI 0.6% for the month and 3.7% for the year; core 0.3% and 2.6%. Both above forecast. Petrol (+9.1% for the month) accounts for more than half of the rise.
What price did
DXY to about 102.8–102.9. EURUSD from about 1.1200 to about 1.1122, the lowest in 16 months. GBPUSD to about 1.3150. Gold −1.5 to −1.9% to about $4,080. The probability of a Fed hike in October jumps to about 42%.
The digestion
The next day EURUSD comes back to about 1.1220. By Friday DXY is about 1% lower on the week. The market decides the jump came mainly from petrol, not from broad inflation.
Visible beforehand
Crowded positioning against the dollar, a clearly described scenario, high oil as the obvious source of a hot headline index.
Lesson
The first reaction trades the number. The second trades its content. Between the two there can be a whole day and the entire move back.

●●29.3 What a gap costs

The SNB dossier seems distant until you calculate your own trade. Your stop is 15 pips. What happens if price jumps 150?

Why scalpers are more exposed

The shorter the stop, the bigger the position for the same risk, and the more painful the gap. A 5-pip stop with a 150-pip gap is thirty times the planned loss. That is why, for a scalper, the calendar is part of the stop.

Gap risk · риск от гапThe risk that price jumps the stop with no fill in between.
Guaranteed stop · гарантиран стопA stop the broker fills exactly at the level for an extra fee. Not everyone offers it.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

A made-up central bank has held a ceiling on its currency for three years: it promises not to let it strengthen past a certain level. This week the governor says the ceiling "remains the foundation of policy". The big neighbouring central banks are just starting to print money. The rate sits five pips from the ceiling. You are thinking of selling the currency with a 10-pip stop, because "it can't get past the ceiling".

Task: which dossier does this remind you of? What is the risk profile of this trade? What should you check before even thinking about it? What does the calculator from 29.3 say if the ceiling breaks with a 300-pip gap?

Sample answer
  1. SNB 2015, almost one to one: a promise of a level, neighbours who are printing, and a confident statement days before.
  2. The profile is "picking up pennies in front of a steamroller": many small profits while the ceiling holds, and one huge loss when it breaks. The probability of a loss on any given day is small, but its size is out of all proportion.
  3. How big the central bank's balance sheet is relative to the economy and how fast it is growing, when the neighbours' next decision is, whether the broker has negative balance protection and whether it offers a guaranteed stop.
  4. With 1% risk and a 10-pip stop, a 300-pip gap is about 30% of the account on one trade. Whatever the probability, that loss is outside every risk rule in this book. The decision itself is mine, but the maths is clear.
