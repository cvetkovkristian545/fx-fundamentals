No single right answer

Sunday evening. A teaching week: FOMC on Wednesday at 21:00 Sofia time, US CPI on Thursday at 15:30. Oil is high because of tension in the straits. The US 10y yield is near a multi-year high, and the real yield is rising. The French spread over Germany is the widest in years, ahead of a rating review on Friday. Retail traders are long EURUSD (~68%) and gold. You are in week 10 of the 90-day plan: 28 demo trades, expectancy +0.4R, 87% of rules followed, the "early-entry" tag is on 5 trades. You are aiming for a 10K prop challenge with a 5% daily and 10% static limit. Your budget for the month is about 75 euros, and you have a credit card with a small debt.

Task: (1) Write the weekly bias for USD, EUR and gold with a probability, a driver and "what kills the idea". (2) Which days and hours are red and why? (3) What does the verdict for EURUSD look like on Thursday at 10:00 if H4 is in a range? (4) What does positioning say? (5) Are you ready for the paid challenge by the criteria from Chapter 34, and what do you do with the budget according to Chapter 38? (6) Which if–then rule do you add this week and why?

Sample answer
  1. USD: leaning up, ~55%, driven by yields and oil; a soft FOMC or a weak CPI kills it. EUR: leaning down, ~55%, driven by the French spread and energy imports; a narrowing spread or a soft dollar kills it. Gold: leaning down, ~55%, driven by rising real yields; a drop in real yields or an escalation that brings rates down kills it. All of it is probability, not a verdict.
  2. Wednesday from about 20:00 until the end of the press conference (21:30 and after): no new positions. Thursday from 15:00 to at least 16:00. Friday around the rating review, if it comes out during trading hours, plus the risk of a gap on Monday.
  3. The direction comes from the bias (down), but H4 is in a range, and CPI is five and a half hours away: yellow, wait for a breakout or a zone at the edge of the range, smaller size. In the middle of the range: red.
  4. The crowd is long EURUSD and gold, opposite to the bias. This adds fuel for a move down on a hot CPI (as in the dossier from 14 October), but also the risk of a sharp squeeze up on a soft FOMC.
  5. Not yet: 28 trades (under 30), 87% of rules followed (under 90%), a recurring early entry. Per Chapter 38, the card is paid off first, and the free trial is used for the challenge. The paid challenge comes after both conditions are met.
  6. "If the entry conditions are met on a closed candle, I enter within 30 seconds; if not, I don't enter and I log it." Because the "early-entry" tag is the most frequent mistake, and the rule removes the decision from the moment. The decisions are yours, but if you answered all six points, you already think like an analyst.
