Psychology and Discipline

We have met the enemy and he is us. Walt Kelly, the "Pogo" comic strip, 1970

After this chapter you will be able to

  • name the traps of the brain that cost money in trading;
  • recognise the four stop conditions: FOMO, revenge, oversized position, no stop;
  • calculate what hesitation at entry costs;
  • turn discipline from willpower into a system of rules.

●35.1 The brain was not built for this

Our brain was built to survive on the savannah, not to trade currencies. It hates losing more than it loves winning, sees patterns where there are none, and believes the last thing it saw. All of this is useful when you are running from a lion. On the chart it is expensive.

TrapWhat it looks like on the chartAntidote
Loss aversionYou move the stop further away so as "not to lose"; you close the profit too earlyThe stop and the target are decided before entry and not touched on emotion
Recency biasThree losses in a row and you think the system doesn't workDecisions only after 20+ trades (Chapter 36)
Confirmation biasYou look only for news that agrees with your positionA "what kills the idea" sentence in the bias file
The gambler's fallacy"After so many losses I'm due a win"Each trade is independent; size is not increased after a loss
Anchoring"I bought at 1.1250, I'll wait for it to come back"The market doesn't know what price you entered at
OverconfidenceAfter a winning streak you double the sizeRisk is 1% in the good weeks too
Mentor's note

Kahneman and Tversky's research shows that a loss usually hurts about twice as much as the joy of a gain of the same size. That is why traders hold losing trades and cut winning ones. The system exists so that you don't ask your feelings when price is 2 pips from the stop.

Loss aversion · неприязън към загубатаA loss weighs more than a gain of equal size.
Tilt · тилтA state in which emotion has taken the wheel. The term comes from poker.
Bias · пристрастиеHere meaning a systematic error in thinking, not a market direction.

●35.2 The four stop conditions

There are four states in which the rule is the same: you close the platform. Not "more carefully", not "just one". You close it.

FOMO

Price left without you and you feel you have to get in right now. A trade you chase is a trade without a setup.

Revenge

You just got stopped out and want your money back. The market owes you nothing.

Oversized position

The risk is above the rule "because this time I'm sure". Certainty is the most expensive feeling in trading.

No stop

A position with no stop, or a stop you "will put in later". That is no longer a trade, it is hope.

    ●●35.3 Hesitation and overanalysis

    There is also the opposite problem, quieter and more common among disciplined people. The setup is there, all the conditions are met, but the hand doesn't click. You wait for one more candle, one more confirmation. When you finally go in, price has run away. Or you don't go in at all, and then watch it reach the target without you.

    Hesitation looks like caution, but it costs money in two ways: missed trades and worse entries. Calculate the second.

    Where it comes from and how to treat it

    CauseSymptomRemedy
    Too many conditionsA 15-point checklist, each "sort of"Few conditions, each with a yes or no answer
    Fear of being wrongEvery loss is experienced as a personal failureA loss within the rules is a cost, not a mistake. A mistake is a broken rule.
    Deciding in the momentYou think about macro when you should be clickingAll decisions are made in the morning; live, you only execute an "if–then" plan
    Too many chartsSix pairs, three timeframes, everything flashingOne or two pairs in the session, alerts on the zones
    The re-entry trap

    You missed the setup. Price took off. Twenty minutes later something appears that looks like a setup in the same direction. Most people go in there to make up for the miss. That is FOMO in a different hat. The rule: a missed trade is logged as missed and the day continues according to plan. The next setup has to pass the full check, not half of it.

    Overanalysis · свръханализLooking for more information after the decision could already be made.
    Hesitation delta · закъснениеThe difference in pips between the planned and the actual entry.
    If-then plan · план ако–тогаваA sentence written in advance: "If X happens, I do Y."

    ●35.4 The system beats willpower

    Willpower is a resource that runs out. In the morning you have it; after eight hours of work and three losses you don't. That is why discipline must not depend on it. It must depend on rules that reduce the number of decisions.

    1. Decisions are made before the session. The zones, the direction, the risk and the news are clear in the morning.
    2. During the session you only execute. If something is not in the plan, the answer is "no".
    3. A trade limit. For example at most two trades per session and done after two losses.
    4. The phone is a tool, not a casino. TradingView alerts yes, charts in bed no. Social media notifications off during the session.
    5. A break after a big event. A big win or a big loss: done for the day. Both cloud judgement.

    StudentIsn't this too strict? Sometimes intuition is right.

    MentorThe intuition of someone with 2,000 logged trades is experience. The intuition of someone with 20 is mood. When your journal shows that the "intuitive" trades win, you will turn them into a rule. Until then they are noise with a nice name.

    Myth

    Good traders have no emotions.

    Reality

    They have the same emotions. The difference is that they have rules that decide for them when the emotion is strong.

    Cognitive load · когнитивно натоварванеHow much mental resource a task requires. The fewer live decisions, the lower the load.
    Variable reward · променлива наградаA reward that comes unpredictably. The mechanism of slot machines and of charts on your phone.
    Debrief

    What you take from Dossier 35

    • The brain hates losing, believes the latest thing and looks for confirmation. The system is the antidote.
    • FOMO, revenge, oversized position, no stop: you close the platform.
    • Hesitation costs missed trades and worse R. Measure it in pips.
    • A missed trade is logged, not chased.
    • Decisions are made before the session; live, you only execute.

    No single right answer

    London, 10:20. The GBPUSD setup is exactly to plan: liquidity taken below the Asian low, displacement up, a return into the zone. You wait for one more M5 candle "to be sure". Price leaves without you and reaches the 3R target in 25 minutes. At 10:50 a weaker setup appears in the same direction, higher up, with a stop that has to be tighter to "make sense". Your pulse is racing.

    Task: which traps and stop conditions do you see? What do you do at 10:50? What do you write in the journal for the first setup? Which "if–then" rule would prevent the hesitation next time?

    Sample answer
    1. First hesitation (overanalysis: a condition that is not in the rules), then FOMO and the re-entry trap. The wish for a tighter stop "to make sense" is a step towards an oversized position.
    2. At 10:50 the pulse and the urge to make up for the miss are a stop condition. The most sensible choice is not to enter and to step away from the screen. If the setup still passes the full check without exceptions, the stop goes where the structure is and the size is calculated from it, never the other way round.
    3. The first setup is logged as "not taken" with the reason: hesitation. Setup score: high. Execution score: low. The two are not mixed. This is the most valuable line in the journal this week.
    4. For example: "If conditions 1–3 are met when the candle closes, I enter within 30 seconds. An extra candle is not a condition." The decision is yours, but the rule has to be so simple that there is nothing to debate live.
