Brokers, Demo Accounts, Platforms

If something sounds too good to be true, it probably is. Folk wisdom, confirmed by every regulator

After this chapter you will be able to

  • check whether a broker is regulated, and by whom;
  • recognise the red flags of scams;
  • choose between MT4, MT5 and cTrader and open a demo account;
  • calculate what one trade costs you and how much of the stop it eats.

●32.1 What "regulated broker" means

The broker holds your money and executes your trades. The regulator is the state body that licenses and supervises it: whether it keeps client money separate from its own, whether it shows fair prices, whether it has enough capital. If the broker has no licence from a serious regulator, there is no one to turn to when something goes wrong.

RegulatorCountryWhat it means for you
FSC (Financial Supervision Commission)BulgariaThe local regulator. Its register also shows the foreign EU firms that are allowed to operate in Bulgaria.
CySECCyprusMany brokers serving Europe are here. EU rules apply in full, and the licence is valid across the EU.
FCAUnited KingdomOne of the strictest. EU clients are usually served by the broker's European subsidiary.
ASICAustraliaA strict regulator. The leverage rules are close to the European ones.
"Offshore" licencesIslands, small jurisdictionsWeak supervision. Often used to offer leverage of 1:500 and more. Protection when something goes wrong is minimal.

For an EU client the ESMA rules are mandatory. The most important for a beginner:

RuleWhat it means
Maximum leverage of 1:30 on major currency pairsFor 1 lot of EURUSD (~100,000 euros) you need about 3,333 euros of margin.
1:20 on other pairs, gold and major indicesLess leverage where volatility is higher.
Forced close-out at 50% marginThe broker closes the positions before you lose everything.
Negative balance protectionYou cannot owe more than the money in the account. You remember SNB 2015 from Chapter 29.
A warning with the percentage of losing clientsEvery EU broker must show how many of its clients lose money. The number is usually between 60 and 80%.
How to check in 5 minutes

Find the licence number at the bottom of the broker's website. Open the regulator's register (the FSC, CySEC or FCA website) and search by number or name. Check that the firm's name and website match those in the register. Scammers often copy a real broker's name with a small difference in the address.

●32.2 Red flags

Tick everything you see at the broker or the "mentor" you are in contact with.

Myth

"My account manager is very kind and helps me."

Reality

At scam brokers the "manager" is a salesperson on commission. Their job is to get you to deposit more. An honest broker does not call you to talk you into taking risk.

Clone firm · клонирана фирмаA scammer using the name and licence of a real regulated broker.
Withdrawal · тегленеTaking money out of the account. The first real test of any broker.

The platform is the program you send orders through. You can do the analysis in TradingView and the execution in the broker's platform, or straight from TradingView if the broker is connected to it.

The recommendation for a beginner is simple: MT5 or cTrader, whichever your broker or prop firm offers. If you are planning a prop challenge, practise on the platform the firm uses.

A demo account in six steps

  1. Choose a regulated broker (32.1) and open a "Demo account". All you need is an email.
  2. Choose a platform (MT5 or cTrader), an account type (see 32.4) and a realistic balance: as much as you would really deposit, or the size of the prop account you are aiming for.
  3. Download the platform or open it in the browser. Log in with the details from the email.
  4. Set the chart time zone if the platform allows it, and add the pairs from your list.
  5. Make one test trade with the minimum size: open, move the stop, close. Learn the buttons before you need them fast.
  6. If the broker is connected to TradingView, log in from the "Trading" panel in TradingView and try an order from there.
The demo trap

A demo with 100,000 dollars when you will really trade with 500 teaches the wrong habits: huge positions, indifference to losses. Set the balance you will use and stick to 1% risk as if the money were real.

●●32.4 Account types and the real cost of a trade

A broker earns in two ways: from the spread (the difference between the buy price and the sell price) and from commission. There are usually two account types.

TypeEURUSD spread (typical)CommissionSuits
Standard0.8–1.5 pipsNoneSwing, few trades
Raw / ECN0.0–0.3 pipsAbout $5–7 per lot for opening and closing combinedScalping and intraday

Typical values are a guide. Check the exact ones with your broker, and at the hour you trade: in Asia and around news the spread is wider.

Mentor's note

For a scalper the cost is part of the stop. With a 6-pip stop and 0.9 pips of cost, every trade starts with a 15% loss of the risk. That is not a reason not to scalp. It is a reason to count.

Lot · лотStandard size: 100,000 units of the base currency. Mini = 0.1, micro = 0.01.
Round turn · пълен цикълOpening plus closing. Commission is often quoted per round turn.
Swap · суапInterest you pay or receive if you hold a position overnight.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 32

  • Only a regulated broker. Check the licence in the register, not on the broker's website.
  • In the EU: leverage up to 1:30 on major pairs and negative balance protection.
  • Guaranteed profits, pressure to deposit and difficult withdrawals are red flags.
  • MT5 or cTrader. Practise on the platform you will use.
  • A demo with a realistic balance and a real 1% risk.
  • The cost of a trade is spread plus commission. For a scalper it is part of the stop.

No single right answer

A scalper trades EURUSD in London with an average stop of 5 pips and about 60 trades a month, at 0.4 lots. Broker A: Standard account, 1.1 pip spread, no commission. Broker B: Raw account, 0.1 pip spread, $7 per lot round trip. Both are regulated by CySEC.

Task: what is the difference in monthly cost? What share of the stop does the cost eat with each? What else would you check besides price?

Sample answer
  1. A: 1.1 pips × $10 × 0.4 = $4.40 per trade, about $264 a month. B: 0.1 pips × $4 = $0.40 spread plus $7 × 0.4 = $2.80 commission, $3.20 per trade in total, about $192 a month. A difference of about $72 a month in B's favour.
  2. A eats 1.1 of 5 pips, that is 22% of the risk on every trade. B eats 0.8 pips, about 16%. Both are significant; with a 5-pip stop the cost is a large share.
  3. The spread at the exact hour of entry (the London open, around news), slippage on stops, execution speed, whether the Raw account has a minimum deposit, swaps if they hold overnight, and how withdrawals go. Price is only the first filter.
