Risk-On, Risk-Off and Intermarket Links

Only when the tide goes out do you discover who's been swimming naked. Warren Buffett

After this chapter you will be able to

  • recognise risk-on and risk-off across currencies, gold, indices, bonds and bitcoin at once;
  • explain the difference between nominal and real yield and why gold listens to the second;
  • connect oil to CAD, copper to AUD and yields to USDJPY;
  • place today's market in one of the four growth × inflation regimes.

●22.1 The market's two moods

Some days the market is bold: it buys stocks, commodity currencies, crypto, anything with higher yield. That is risk-on. Other days it is scared: it runs to dollars, yen, francs, gold and government bonds. That is risk-off. Most assets move together with this mood, so it is worth reading it before you look at your own pair.

●●22.2 Bonds: price and yield are on a seesaw

A bond is a loan with a fixed payment. If someone sells bonds, their price falls and the yield for the new buyer rises. If everyone buys, the price rises and the yield falls. Price and yield always move in opposite directions.

Why does a currency trader care? Because government bond yields are "the price of money" for the whole economy. The long yield (10 and 30 years) sets mortgages and company loans. When US 10-year yields rise sharply, the dollar usually strengthens, while gold, stocks and crypto get cheaper.

Myth

Bonds are boring and do not affect currencies.

Reality

The bond market is bigger than the stock market and often leads. Many experienced macro traders look at yields first and currencies second.

Bond · облигацияA loan a state or company takes against fixed payments.
Yield · доходностThe annual return for the buyer at the current price.

●●22.3 Nominal and real yields; real yields and gold

The nominal yield is the number you see. The real yield is the nominal minus expected inflation. If a bond pays 4% and inflation is expected at 3%, you earn about 1% in real terms.

Gold pays no interest. When the real yield rises, the opportunity cost of holding gold rises and gold usually gets cheaper. When the real yield falls (especially below zero), gold usually gets dearer. It is the most reliable medium-term link for XAUUSD, though it is not a law.

Mentor's note

The 2026 notes describe a period in which gold fell during war headlines. The logic "war → gold up" flips because war lifts oil, oil lifts inflation and rate expectations, and real yields rise. The headline says one thing, the yield seesaw says another. Listen to the seesaw.

An exception: when gold and the dollar rise together. That is not rate logic but fear for the system itself: sanctions, sovereign debt, central banks buying gold. Chapter 24 returns to central bank gold buying.

●●●22.5 The four regimes: growth × inflation

Lay out the environment on two axes: growth above or below expectations and inflation above or below expectations. Each quadrant has typical winners. Click a quadrant.

How do you know which quadrant you are in? Growth: PMI, NFP, retail sales, jobless claims. Inflation: CPI, PCE, commodity prices, inflation expectations. A regime change shows up first in the surprises and in the yield curve (Chapter 19). You stick with the regime until the data change it.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 22

  • Risk-on: stocks, commodity currencies, crypto up. Risk-off: USD, JPY, CHF, gold, bonds up.
  • Bond price and yield are on a seesaw.
  • Real yields lead gold; the headline can lie, the seesaw rarely does.
  • Oil → CAD/NOK; copper → AUD; US yields → USDJPY.
  • The four regimes say which assets win; the regime is changed by surprises.

No single right answer

A day of geopolitical escalation in the Middle East. Oil +6%. The US 10y yield +12 bp because the market expects more inflation. Gold, instead of rising, falls 1.5%. S&P −1.2%. USDJPY +0.8%. AUDUSD −0.9%. USDCAD almost unchanged.

Task: risk-on or risk-off? Why does gold fall on a day like this? Why does USDJPY rise even though the yen is a safe haven? Why does USDCAD stand still? What does this say for EURUSD?

Sample answer
  1. Risk-off by stocks and AUD, but with an inflationary flavour: oil and yields up.
  2. Gold falls because real yields rise: the nominal yield jumps more than expected inflation, or the market expects a more hawkish Fed. The seesaw beats the headline.
  3. USDJPY rises because the yield differential widens and Japan imports energy: dearer oil is bad for the yen. The two effects outweigh its safe-haven role.
  4. USDCAD stands still because two effects cancel out: a strong dollar (risk-off, yields) versus strong oil (support for CAD).
  5. For EURUSD the wind is more likely down: a stronger dollar, and Europe is an energy importer. The verdict depends on structure and the calendar, but the fundamental wind is against the euro.
