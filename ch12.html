Managing an Open Position

Cut your losses short and let your profits run. Old stock-market maxim, linked to David Ricardo

After this chapter you will be able to

  • plan a target of at least 3R with a partial close and a stop moved to breakeven;
  • explain why the realised result is often under 3R and why that is normal;
  • name the three things you never do with an open position.

●12.1 The plan: TP1, breakeven, final target

Position management is decided before entry, not during it. Otherwise every twitch of price becomes a reason for a new emotion. The standard plan in this book's framework has three parts:

  1. TP1: the first logical liquidity in the direction (the previous high, the Asian low, PDH). Half the position is closed there.
  2. Breakeven: after TP1 the stop goes to the entry price. From that moment the trade can't end in a loss (except through slippage).
  3. TP2: the next structural zone or the big liquidity. The plan aims for at least 3R in total.
TP1 / TP2The first and second profit targets.
Partial close · частично затварянеYou close part of the position; the rest carries on.
Breakeven (BE) · нулаThe stop is at the entry price: the trade can no longer lose.

●12.2 Why the realised result is under 3R

You plan 3R, but in the journal you will see many +1Rs and zeros. That isn't the plan failing. That is the plan working. The partial close and breakeven turn some future losses into small wins or zeros. The price is that sometimes you exit early from a trade that later goes a long way.

The book's rule: a trade is planned for at least 3R; closing below 3R is allowed when the plan calls for it (TP1 + breakeven) or a new circumstance does (see 12.4). Shifting the plan out of emotion is not allowed.

●12.3 The three “nevers”

Never 1

Don't widen the stop. The stop is where the scenario is wrong. If you move it further away, you are simply refusing to accept you were wrong, and paying more for it.

Never 2

Don't chase the target. You don't move the TP further because “it's going nicely”. The target was liquidity; that is where someone closes their positions against you.

Never 3

Don't add to a losing position. If the scenario isn't working, a bigger size won't fix it.

StudentBut if I remove the stop for a bit, price might come back.

MentorIt might. Most accounts that vanished in a single day vanished right after that sentence.

Averaging down · усредняванеAdding to a losing position. Forbidden in this framework.

●●12.4 An open position and news

Sometimes a position is open and a red folder is approaching on the calendar. The protocol in this book's framework:

  1. In profit: consider a partial close (50%) before the news.
  2. The stop can be tightened to the last significant low or high if that is sensible, or to breakeven if the position allows.
  3. Never widen the stop before news.
  4. Don't add to the position in the post-news spike, even if it is in your favour. Wait 15–30 minutes and assess.
Watch on Forex Factory

Before opening a position, check not just the next hour but the next 4 hours. If there is a red folder there, the management plan must include it from the start.

Myth

Breakeven is perfect protection; I move the stop to breakeven as soon as I can.

Reality

A breakeven set too early turns many good trades into zeros, because a normal pullback touches it. In the framework it comes after TP1, not after the first 5 pips.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 12

  • A plan before entry: TP1 (partial), breakeven, TP2. At least 3R in total.
  • Realised R is often under 3R. That is allowed when the plan says so.
  • Never: a wider stop, a more distant target, adding to a loser.
  • Before news: a partial close or a tightened stop; never a widened one.

No single right answer

Long EURUSD from 1.0815, stop 1.0803 (12 pips), TP1 1.0839 (2R) at the Asian high, TP2 1.0863 (4R) at PDH. Price reached 1.0839, half was closed and the stop is at 1.0815. It is now 15:10, NFP is at 15:30, price is 1.0846.

Task: what do you do with the remaining half before NFP? Describe two sensible actions and one unacceptable one. What is the result in R for each exit?

Sample answer
  1. +1R is already locked in (half at 2R). The rest is at breakeven.
  2. Option 1: you close the rest at 1.0846 before NFP. The rest gives 31 ÷ 12 ≈ 2.6R; in total 0.5 × 2 + 0.5 × 2.6 ≈ +2.3R. Under 3R and fully allowed: the news is a new circumstance.
  3. Option 2: you leave the rest with the stop at breakeven (or tightened below 1.0839). Worst case: about +1R in total. Best case: TP2 and +3R in total. You risk slippage in the spike.
  4. Unacceptable: removing the stop “so the spike doesn't knock you out”, or adding to the position in the spike.
