Liquidity and SMC Zones

If you've been in the game 30 minutes and you don't know who the patsy is, you're the patsy. Old poker saying, popularised by Warren Buffett

After this chapter you will be able to

  • point out where the stops sit on the chart: BSL, SSL, equal highs and lows, Asia, PDH, PDL;
  • draw an order block and a Fair Value Gap and say which zone is stronger;
  • split the range into an expensive and a cheap half (premium and discount).

●7.1 Where the stops are

Before this chapter

If you are on the fast track, this chapter belongs to Stage 4. Pass your first backtest with a simple setup, then use the ideas here as filters you test one at a time (0.3.8).

In Chapter 0.2 you saw that price moves to where the orders are. The biggest pile of orders on any chart is other traders' stops. Everyone who has sold has a stop above some high. Everyone who has bought has a stop below some low. A seller's stop is a buy order; a buyer's stop is a sell order.

LiquidityWhereWhose orders
BSL (buy-side)Above highsSellers' stops + breakout buy orders = buy orders
SSL (sell-side)Below lowsBuyers' stops + breakout sell orders = sell orders
Equal highs / lowsTwo or three highs at one priceThe most obvious place for a stop, so the most attractive target
Asian rangeThe high and low of the overnight sessionThe stops of everyone who traded the night
PDH / PDLThe high and low of the previous dayDaily targets for London and New York

A big player who wants to buy a billion euros needs someone to sell them a billion. Where will they find that many sellers at once? Below the obvious low, where thousands of buyers' stops will turn into sell orders. That is why price so often first makes a false move towards liquidity and only then sets off in the real direction.

BSL · ликвидност отгореBuy-side liquidity: buy orders above the highs.
SSL · ликвидност отдолуSell-side liquidity: sell orders below the lows.
EQH / EQLEqual highs / lows. A magnet for price.

●7.2 Sweep: the grab before the real move

A sweep is a fast move beyond a level with liquidity, followed by a return. On the chart it looks like a wick above the high or below the low, with the candle closing back inside. It is the most common way real moves begin.

Mentor's note

Your stop is no secret. It's where anyone would put it. If you want to survive, put it where your scenario is wrong, not where it's convenient.

Sweep / liquidity grabA quick move beyond a level with liquidity and back again.
Stop hunt · лов на стоповеA colloquial name for a sweep.

●●7.3 Order block and Fair Value Gap

An order block (OB) is the last opposite-direction candle before a strong move. A bullish OB is the last red candle before the strong move up; a bearish OB is the last green candle before the strong move down. The idea: that is where the big player built their position. When price returns, they often defend it. A rule from practice: decisions in an OB are taken in the lower 75% of the candle (for bullish), not at its top end, because from there the stop below the zone is furthest away and the reward-to-risk ratio gets worse.

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is the imbalance from Chapter 0.2, measured precisely. Take three consecutive candles. If the wick of the first and the wick of the third don't overlap, the empty space between them is an FVG. Price passed through so fast that there were almost no trades. The market often comes back to “fill” it.

POI (Point of Interest) is the general name for any zone where you expect a reaction. Not all are equal. The ranking in this book, from strongest to weakest:

  1. An OB and an FVG that overlap. Two reasons in one place.
  2. An OB formed after a sweep. Liquidity has been taken, the position has been built.
  3. A clean FVG in the right half of the range.
  4. A level with liquidity plus a structural level (S/R from Chapter 4).
OB · order blockThe last opposite-direction candle before a strong impulse.
FVG · Fair Value GapA gap between the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 in a strong move.
POI · зона на интересA place where you expect price to react.
Mitigation · връщане в зонаPrice comes back to “touch” an OB or FVG.

●●7.4 Premium and discount: expensive and cheap

Take the last big move, from SSL to BSL. That is the dealing range. Its middle, 50%, is equilibrium. The upper half is premium (expensive): it makes more sense to look for sells there. The lower half is discount (cheap): it makes more sense to look for buys there. Buying in premium means you pay up and your stop is far away.

The typical sequence in a range is: sweep of SSL → climb → sweep of BSL → reversal, or the reverse. Price “breathes” between the two pools of liquidity.

Myth

Buy when price has fallen a lot, because it's cheap.

Reality

“Cheap” makes sense only in context: discount of a range in a bullish trend, after liquidity has been taken. Without context “cheap” can get a lot cheaper.

Dealing range · диапазонThe move between the last significant liquidity below and above.
Premium · скъпа зонаThe upper half of the range.
Discount · евтина зонаThe lower half of the range.
Equilibrium · равновесие50% of the range.

●●●Go deeperWhat a stop hunt really is▾

Nobody at a bank is looking at your stop. What the big players see is where orders are likely to cluster: above equal highs, below round numbers, beyond yesterday's high. They care because they need liquidity: a fund that wants to buy 500 million euros cannot do it at one price unless many people are selling at the same moment. Triggered sell stops are exactly that.

So a "hunt" is usually not a conspiracy. It is a large order looking for the other side, plus short-term traders who know where the stops are and push price there because the fill is guaranteed. The exception is a B-book broker, who does see client stops, and that is one more reason to know your broker's model.

What this means for you: a stop placed exactly where everyone else's is will be filled in the sweep. Place it where your idea is wrong, beyond the obvious pool, and size down to keep the risk at 1%.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 07

  • Liquidity sits above highs (BSL) and below lows (SSL). Equal highs and lows are the strongest magnets.
  • A sweep is a wick beyond liquidity with a close back inside; it is often the start of the real move.
  • An OB is the last opposite candle before the impulse. An FVG is the gap between candle 1 and candle 3.
  • POI strength: OB+FVG > OB after a sweep > clean FVG > level + liquidity.
  • Buy in discount, sell in premium, always in the context of the higher timeframe.

EURUSD H1, D1 is bullish. Range: SSL 1.0780, BSL 1.0900. Asia is between 1.0812 and 1.0830. In London price falls to 1.0806 and closes back above 1.0812, then prints a strong green candle that leaves an FVG between 1.0818 and 1.0824. The last red candle before it is 1.0809–1.0815.

Task: name the sweep, the OB and the FVG. Which half of the range are we in? Which is the strongest zone to watch if price comes back? Where is the next liquidity above?

Sample answer
  1. Sweep of the Asian SSL: a wick to 1.0806, a close above 1.0812.
  2. OB: the last red candle, 1.0809–1.0815. FVG: 1.0818–1.0824. They don't overlap but are close; the OB comes after a sweep, which makes it stronger.
  3. Range equilibrium: (1.0780 + 1.0900) ÷ 2 = 1.0840. Price is below it: discount. With a bullish D1 this is the logical place to watch for buys.
  4. Strongest zone: the OB after the sweep (1.0809–1.0815), its lower 75%. The scenario is invalidated by a close below the sweep's low (1.0806).
  5. Liquidity above: the Asian high at 1.0830, then BSL at 1.0900.
