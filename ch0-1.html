●0.1.1 What this book is

Start here

New to trading? Don't read all 41 chapters in a row. Follow the fast track in 0.3: a short core, then your first backtest, then the advanced chapters.

This book is a map. It shows what to watch on the chart, how to analyse what you see and how to read the news that moves price. It starts from zero: what a buyer is, what a seller is, and why the price of bread, petrol and the euro changes by the same logic. It goes as far as things many traders with years of experience never use: order flow, central banks and geopolitics.

This book is not a signal service. There is no “buy here, sell there” in it. There is no secret strategy that always wins, because no such thing exists. You will find frameworks, checklists and sample analyses. They are a way of thinking, not trade instructions.

Mentor's note

A book cannot teach you to trade, just as a book about swimming cannot teach you to swim. It can, however, tell you where the water is deep, where the current is and why you don't jump in right after a meal.

Myth

Trading is a quick way to get rich. All you need is the right strategy.

Reality

Trading is a skill built over months and years. Strategy is a small part of it. Risk, discipline and habits decide more.

●0.1.2 Risk warning

Read this twice

Most retail traders lose money. Brokers in the EU must publish what percentage of client accounts lose money trading CFDs. That figure is usually between 60% and 80%. Leverage (explained in Chapter 2) multiplies both profits and losses. Nothing in this book is investment advice. Trade only with money you can afford to lose completely.

We say this at the start because it is the most important sentence in the book. Everything after it is about thinking clearly in an environment where most people fail. The good news is that the reasons they fail are known, and most are behavioural: too much risk, no plan, trading on emotion. These things can be learned.

No single right answer

A friend shows you a Telegram group where a “mentor” sells signals for 99 euros a month and posts screenshots of 400% profits in a week. Your friend wants to subscribe today and put in their salary.

Task: what questions would you ask them? What from this chapter contradicts what they are seeing? What would you suggest instead?

Sample answer
  1. Do the screenshots show the losses too? Is there a verifiable third-party track record of at least a year?
  2. If the signals make 400% a week, why is this person selling subscriptions for 99 euros instead of trading?
  3. How much of the salary can you lose without it affecting your life? A salary is not risk capital (Chapter 38).
  4. Signals don't teach. When they stop, you are left without a skill. The book teaches you to read the market yourself.
  5. Suggestion: 4 weeks of reading and backtesting, then demo with a journal. Zero euros, zero risk.
