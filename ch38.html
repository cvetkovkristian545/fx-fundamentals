Banks and Taxes in Bulgaria

In this world nothing can be said to be certain, except death and taxes. Benjamin Franklin

After this chapter you will be able to

  • move money to a broker and back without problems with the bank;
  • understand how profits from currencies, CFDs, crypto, stocks and dividends are taxed;
  • roughly calculate your tax for the year;
  • know the deadlines and what to keep.
Not tax advice

This chapter describes the general rules in Bulgaria as of 21 October 2026 for income received in 2026 and declared in 2027, under the Personal Income Tax Act (ЗДДФЛ) and the public guides for filling in the return. The codes in the return follow the 2025 form; the National Revenue Agency publishes the new form at the start of the year. For larger sums, prop payouts, staking or many trades, consult an accountant.

●41.1 Money to the broker and back

Since 1 January 2026 Bulgaria is in the eurozone. Accounts, salaries and transfers are in euros, and SEPA transfers to a broker in the EU are usually fast and cheap.

  1. The same account in and out. Brokers usually pay out through the same channel you deposited with. This is an anti-money-laundering rule.
  2. A euro account at the broker, if possible. Every conversion into dollars costs a fee and a spread.
  3. The bank may ask about the origin of the money for larger or unusual transfers. Keep the broker statements.
  4. Prop firms pay out by bank transfer, through payment systems or in crypto. Check the fees and keep every document.
  5. Keep everything for five years or more: annual statements, trade confirmations, bank transfers.
SEPAThe Single Euro Payments Area. Transfers within it are like domestic ones.

Profits and losses from currencies, CFDs, crypto and taxable stocks go into one general base for the year. A CFD loss reduces a crypto profit. Losses are not carried forward to later years.

The exchange rate

Every amount in dollars or another currency is converted into euros at the Bulgarian National Bank (BNB) reference rate for the date of the trade, not at the broker's rate.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 41

  • You deposit and withdraw through the same channel. You keep the statements.
  • Currencies, CFDs and crypto: one general base, 10% after 10% expenses, no carrying forward of losses.
  • Stocks and ETFs on a regulated market in the EU/EEA: 0%. The execution venue decides.
  • Dividends: 5%, with foreign tax credited up to 5%.
  • The BNB rate for the date of the trade. Deadline: 30 April; a discount for filing by 31 March.
  • Prop payouts, staking and high-volume trading: an accountant.

No single right answer

In 2026: +3,100 € from closed CFDs on EURUSD, −1,900 € from other CFDs, +800 € from selling ETH for USDC, −300 € from selling an altcoin, 0 € withdrawn to the bank from crypto. You sold an ETF on NASDAQ at a 400 € profit and an ETF on Xetra at a 250 € profit. You received 120 € of US dividends with 15% withheld, and one payout from a prop firm.

Task: what is taxable and what is not? What is the rough tax? What do you do with the prop payout?

Sample answer
  1. Taxable in the general base: the CFDs (+3,100 and −1,900), the ETH-for-USDC swap (+800, taxable even without a withdrawal in euros), the altcoin (−300) and the NASDAQ ETF (+400, outside the EU/EEA). The Xetra ETF (+250) is exempt: a regulated market in the EU.
  2. Base: 3,100 − 1,900 + 800 − 300 + 400 = 2,100 €. Tax: 2,100 × 0.9 × 10% ≈ 189 €.
  3. Dividends: 5% of 120 = 6 €; 15% withheld = 18 €, but at most 6 € is credited. Additional: 0 €. The excess taken in the US is not refunded by Bulgaria.
  4. The prop payout is a grey area: it is not a profit from your trade. It is sensible to talk to an accountant before filing and to keep the contract and every payment. The decision is yours, but a guess is not a strategy.
