Personal Finance First

Do not save what is left after spending, but spend what is left after saving. Attributed to Warren Buffett

After this chapter you will be able to

  • split your income using the 50/30/20 rule;
  • calculate your emergency fund;
  • decide whether to pay off debt or invest first;
  • define your risk capital: money you can lose without it changing your life.

●38.1 Why this chapter is in a trading book

The worst trader is the one who needs the trade to win. Needing the rent, the instalment, the electricity bill turns every stop into a catastrophe and every wobble into panic. That is why personal finance comes before trading, not after it. Your trading account must be money that can disappear without you changing anything in your life.

The order is simple:

  1. Budget. You know where the money goes.
  2. A small emergency buffer. One month of expenses.
  3. Expensive debt. Credit cards, payday loans.
  4. A full emergency fund. 3–6 months of expenses.
  5. Long-term investments. Chapter 39.
  6. Risk capital. Only here does trading come in.
Risk capital · рисков капиталMoney whose loss does not affect your bills, savings or plans.
Emergency fund · авариен фондMoney for unexpected expenses: illness, repairs, losing your job.

●38.2 The 50/30/20 budget

The rule is a starting point, not a law: 50% of net income for needs (housing, food, transport, bills), 30% for wants, 20% for savings and paying off debt. If your needs are above 50%, don't blame yourself; just know that the trading account will grow more slowly.

Net income · нетен доходPay after taxes and social contributions.
Pay yourself first · плати първо на себе сиSavings are transferred on payday, not at the end of the month.

●38.3 The emergency fund and debt

The emergency fund is boring. That is exactly why it works. It sits in a savings account or a deposit, easy to reach, not in stocks and not in crypto. In Bulgaria deposits of up to 100,000 euros per person per bank are guaranteed by the Bank Deposit Guarantee Fund.

Debt is the flip side of investment. A credit card at 20% a year is a guaranteed 20% loss. No strategy guarantees you a 20% profit. That is why paying off expensive debt is the best safe "investment" you have.

StudentBut if I pass a prop challenge, I'll pay off the debt in a month.

MentorIf. The debt charges interest every day, and the challenge doesn't know you. People who trade to pay off debt trade under pressure, and pressure is the most expensive indicator. Debt first, then the market.

APR / ГПРThe annual percentage rate of a loan: its real cost, including fees.
ФГВБThe Bulgarian Bank Deposit Guarantee Fund.

●●38.4 Compound interest and risk capital

Einstein probably never said compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world. But the maths is right: small regular contributions and time do more than big bets.

Risk capital is defined last: what is left once the budget, the buffer, the debt and the long-term contributions are covered. If 50 euros a month is left, your trading account is 50 euros a month. That is normal. That is why prop firms (Chapter 33) are interesting for people with little capital: the fee is the risk capital, not the whole account.

Mentor's note

A prop challenge paid for from the emergency fund is not an investment in yourself. It is a bet with the money for a flat tyre. Markets and tyres have a bad habit of blowing out in the same month.

Compound interest · сложна лихваInterest on interest: the profit starts earning by itself.
DCA · усредняванеDollar-cost averaging: investing the same amount regularly, whatever the price.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 38

  • A trader who needs the profit trades under pressure.
  • Order: budget, buffer, expensive debt, full fund, long-term investments, risk capital.
  • 50/30/20 is a starting point.
  • The emergency fund is 3–6 months of needs, somewhere safe.
  • Expensive debt is paid off before any investment.
  • Risk capital is what is left at the end.

No single right answer

Your net income is 1,300 euros. Needs are 750, wants 350. You have 1,800 euros of credit card debt at 21% a year and 500 euros saved. This month you want to pay for a 10K prop challenge for about 90 euros and put 100 euros a month into an ETF.

Task: order the priorities. How long will each step take? When is it sensible to pay for the first challenge?

Sample answer
  1. 200 euros a month are left. If wants shrink to 250, 300 are left.
  2. First a buffer of about 750 (one month of needs): another 250, about a month. Then the card: 1,800 at 300 a month is about 6–7 months with the interest. Then the emergency fund up to 3–4 months of needs.
  3. The ETF contributions start after the card. Until then a guaranteed 21% loss beats any expected return.
  4. The challenge: during these months, free trials and demo (Chapter 34). The first paid challenge is sensible when the card is paid off and the criteria from Chapter 34 are met. The decision is yours, but the buffer money is not risk capital.
