Central Banks and Interest Rates

Within our mandate, the ECB is ready to do whatever it takes to preserve the euro. And believe me, it will be enough. Mario Draghi, July 2012

After this chapter you will be able to

  • explain what a central bank does and why it is independent;
  • trace how a rate decision reaches the currency, loans and prices;
  • tell a hawkish tone from a dovish one and read the market's reaction in stages;
  • know the main banks: the Fed, ECB, BoJ, BoE, SNB, RBA, RBNZ, BoC, and what happened to the BNB.

●18.1 What a central bank does

The central bank is the bank of the banks and of the state. It issues the money, holds the reserves, supervises the banks and, most importantly for you, sets the price of money: the policy rate.

Every bank has a mandate, a task set by law. The Fed has a dual mandate: stable prices and maximum employment. The ECB, the BoE and most others focus mainly on price stability, usually inflation around 2%. The mandate tells you which number the bank watches most closely, and so which number will move its currency.

Why is the central bank independent? Because politicians face elections every 2–4 years, and inflation is a quiet tax that is tempting to use. An independent bank can raise rates when it is unpopular. When politicians start leaning on the bank, the market adds a risk premium to the currency. Turkey in recent years is the textbook example.

Mentor's note

A central bank does not control the economy. It controls one price and tries to influence expectations. Sometimes it succeeds. Sometimes, like the Fed in 1931, it raises rates in the depths of a depression. They are people with models, not oracles.

●●18.2 Transmission: how the rate reaches everything

When the bank raises rates, the effect travels along several paths. Some are fast (the currency reacts in seconds), others slow (shop prices change after 6 to 18 months). That time gap is why central banks are often late.

●●18.3 The tools: the rate, QE and QT, the words

Policy rate

The main lever. In the US it is announced as a range (for example 4.25–4.50%), in the euro area it is the deposit rate.

QE

The bank buys bonds with newly created money. Long-term rates fall, liquidity rises, the currency usually weakens.

QT

The reverse: the bank lets bonds mature without buying new ones, or sells them. Supports the currency but can drain liquidity.

Forward guidance

Words about the future. Often stronger than the decision itself, because they change expectations months ahead.

An example of the power of words: in May 2013 Fed Chair Ben Bernanke merely hinted that bond purchases might be reduced. Markets around the world lost trillions and yields jumped. It is remembered as the taper tantrum. There was no decision. There was a sentence.

Hawkish or dovish? Try it

Read the sentences and decide what the tone is. Do not think about the decision itself, but about the direction it suggests.

●●●18.4 The Taylor rule and r* in plain words

What "should" the rate be? The economist John Taylor proposed a simple formula: start from the neutral rate (r*, the one that neither speeds up nor slows down the economy), add inflation, then add half the gap between inflation and the target and half the output gap (how far the economy is above or below its capacity).

i = r* + π + 0.5 × (π − π*) + 0.5 × (gap)

Why do you need this? If the actual rate is well below "Taylor", the market suspects the bank is behind the curve and will have to catch up with hikes. That is a bullish signal for the currency if the bank is credible.

r* · неутрална лихваThe real rate at which policy neither stimulates nor restricts.
Output gap · производствен разривThe difference between actual and potential GDP.
Behind the curveThe bank has been late in reacting.

The rule you will use most often

A pair is two banks. EURUSD is the ECB versus the Fed. What matters is not only whether the Fed hikes. What matters is which bank is more hawkish than expected relative to the other. If the Fed is more hawkish and the ECB more dovish than expected, EURUSD is more likely to fall.

FOMCThe Fed committee that decides on rates. Meets 8 times a year.
Divergence · разминаванеTwo banks going in different directions. A main driver of the pair.

●●18.6 How the market reacts: decision, statement, press conference

The decision is rarely a surprise. The market has "priced" it through futures weeks before. So the move comes from the gap between what was expected and what was said. The reaction has four phases:

Days beforePre-positioningThe market buys or sells according to expectations. Often "buy the rumour, sell the fact".
In the minuteInstant spikeDecision and statement. Algorithms read the words and react in seconds. The spread widens.
30 min laterThe press conferenceThe chair answers questions. The tone here often reverses the first move.
Hours and daysTrend or reversalThe market digests and positions for the coming months. This is often where the real move starts.

Protocol

A central bank decision is a top-risk event: ±60 minutes with no new positions, and the press conference is guarded in full (Chapter 13). The analysis here is so you understand which way the wind blows after the event, not so you can bet in the minute.

●18.7 The BNB, the currency board and the euro

After the 1996–1997 crisis (Chapter 17) Bulgaria introduced a currency board on 1 July 1997. The lev was pegged to the German mark, and from 1999 to the euro: 1.95583 leva per 1 euro. Every lev in circulation was backed by reserves. The BNB could not print money at will and had no interest rate policy of its own. Rates in Bulgaria effectively followed the euro area.

Since 1 January 2026 Bulgaria has been a member of the euro area. The lev was replaced by the euro at the same rate, the currency board ended, and the BNB governor sits on the ECB Governing Council. For you as a trader this means ECB decisions are now decisions about your own currency, and EURUSD is your pair in the most literal sense.

StudentSo the BNB doesn't matter any more?

MentorIt has a vote at the table where decisions are made. Before, there was neither a table nor a vote, only a fixed rate. The influence is small, but it is more than zero.

Currency board · валутен бордA fixed rate fully backed by reserves, with no monetary policy of its own.
Eurosystem · ЕвросистемаThe ECB plus the national central banks of the euro countries.
●●●Go deeperReading the probabilities the market prices▾

Before every meeting you will read headlines like "the market prices a 70% chance of a cut". The number comes from futures and swaps on the overnight rate. For the Fed, the fed funds futures price is 100 minus the expected average rate for that month: a price of 96.10 means about 3.90%.

A simplified calculation: if the current rate is 4.00% and the futures for the month after the meeting imply 3.90%, the market prices 0.10 of a 0.25 cut, about 40%. Tools like CME FedWatch do this properly, adjusting for the meeting date inside the month.

Why it matters: the reaction depends on the decision relative to this probability. A cut priced at 95% moves little. A cut priced at 40% is a surprise. Check the probability the day before, not after.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 18

  • The central bank sets the price of money and tries to manage expectations.
  • The mandate says which number the bank watches; that number moves its currency.
  • The currency channel is the fastest; inflation reacts after 6–18 months.
  • QE usually weakens the currency, QT supports it; words often weigh more than the decision.
  • A pair is two banks: you trade the difference in tone relative to expectations.
  • The reaction comes in phases; the press conference often reverses the first move.
  • Since 2026 Bulgaria is in the euro area: the ECB decides for your money too.

Wednesday, FOMC. The market has priced a 90% chance of a 0.25 pp cut. At 21:00 the Fed cuts by 0.25, but the statement says "risks to inflation remain to the upside", and the dot plot shows only one more cut next year instead of three. EURUSD first jumps 25 pips, then within 10 minutes comes back and falls below the pre-decision level. At 21:30 the press conference starts.

Task: what is this type of decision called? Why did EURUSD first jump and then fall? What would you watch during the press conference? What is sensible behaviour for you tonight and tomorrow morning in London?

Sample answer
  1. This is a hawkish cut: the decision is dovish, but the path ahead is more hawkish than expected.
  2. The first jump is a reaction to the headline "cut". Then algorithms and people read the dot plot: fewer cuts than priced. The dollar strengthens, EURUSD falls.
  3. During the press conference: whether the chair confirms or softens the dot plot, words like "patience", "data", "no rush". If they soften it, the move can reverse again.
  4. Tonight: no new positions (the decision protocol and the whole press conference). Tomorrow in London: the new fundamental wind is a stronger dollar; you check whether the EURUSD D1/H4 structure confirms with a BOS down. If the structure is still bullish, the verdict is YELLOW and size is cut or the trade skipped.
