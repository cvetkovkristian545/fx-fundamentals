Journal and Measurement

What gets measured gets improved. Attributed to Peter Drucker

After this chapter you will be able to

  • keep a journal that also records missed setups;
  • score the setup and the execution separately, without mixing them;
  • tag your mistakes so you can count them;
  • calculate your statistics and know when the sample is too small for conclusions.

●36.1 What gets recorded

The journal is not a diary of feelings and not a list of profits. It is a database from which, months from now, you will find out what works. That is why every entry has the same fields. They are filled in right after closing, not at the end of the week: memory is the worst archive.

●●36.2 Two scores, never mixed

In Chapter 10 you scored the analysis out of 25 before the session. The journal has two more scores, each out of 10: how good the setup was and how well you executed. They are never added into one number. If you mix them, a good trade with poor execution and a poor trade with good execution look the same, while their lessons are opposite. Click in the quadrants.

How to score execution

Start from 10 and subtract: −2 for an entry outside the plan (early or late), −2 for a stop moved on emotion, −2 for a size outside the rule, −2 for an exit outside the plan, −2 for a trade near news without a plan for it. If you followed everything, execution is 10, even if the trade lost.

Setup score · оценка на сетъпаThe quality of the opportunity, regardless of the result.
Execution score · оценка на изпълнениетоHow precisely the plan was followed.

●●36.3 Tags: counting mistakes

Free text is nice to read, but it can't be counted. Tags can. After 30 trades you can say "early entries cost me 4R this month" instead of "sometimes I go in early".

Mentor's note

The "data-missing" tag sounds bureaucratic, but it is the most useful one. A trade with no entry, stop and target cannot be scored, and a score built on missing data is fiction with percentages.

Tag · етикетA short word that lets you count identical cases.
Horizon drift · изместване на хоризонтаA position opened as short-term that is held much longer than planned.

●●36.4 Statistics and the threshold of 20

Enter your results in R, separated by commas. For example: 3, −1, −1, 2.5, −1, 3.2, 0.

EntriesWhat you can say
10Whether you fill in the fields. Nothing about the strategy.
20A first rough look. All conclusions are provisional.
30The minimum for deciding on the next step (Chapter 34).
50You can compare sessions, pairs, tags. Still with caution.
100+The statistics start to mean something stable.
Myth

"At conviction 5 out of 5 I win 93% of trades, so I should risk more on the fives."

Reality

If that number rests on 15 trades (14 wins), it is provisional, not a rule. Two new losses bring it down to 82%. The conclusion is drawn after 20, and size is changed after many more.

Win rate · процент печелившиHow many of the trades are winners.
Expectancy · очакванеThe average result per trade in R.
Losing streak · губеща серияConsecutive losses. It decides how much limit you need.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 36

  • The journal is a database: the same fields, filled in immediately.
  • Missed setups get recorded.
  • Analysis /25, setup /10, execution /10: never mixed.
  • Tags turn mistakes into numbers.
  • Under 20 entries, no conclusions; decisions after 30; stability after 100.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

A journal with 23 trades: 13 wins, 10 losses, average win 2.1R. The "early-entry" tag is on 7 trades, 6 of them losses. The "below-3R" tag is on 9 trades. At conviction 5/5 there are 6 trades, all winners. The trader wants to raise the risk on the fives to 2%.

Task: what is the expectancy? What is the biggest fix in the journal? What do you say about the 2% risk idea? What will you check at 50 entries?

Sample answer
  1. 13 × 2.1 = 27.3R of wins, 10 × 1 = 10R of losses. Net 17.3R over 23 trades, about +0.75R per trade. Good, but provisional.
  2. Early entries: 6 losses out of 7. Had those trades not been taken, the net result would be about +5R better. That is an execution problem, cured with an "if–then" rule, not with a new strategy. The second theme: 9 trades below 3R, meaning targets are being cut.
  3. 6 trades are far below the threshold of 20. Six out of six can be chance. Risk stays at 1%. Increasing size is discussed if, after 20+ trades at conviction 5, the difference holds.
  4. At 50: whether expectancy improves without the early entries, whether the fives still stand out, which session and pair hold the edge, and how long the longest losing streak is relative to the prop account limit. The conclusions remain theirs, but the numbers will be more serious.
