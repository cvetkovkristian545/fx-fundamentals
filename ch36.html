●39.1 The four main asset classes

AssetWhat you ownWhere the income comes fromThe main risk
StockA small part of a companyEarnings growth, dividendsThe company fails; drops of 30–50% in crises
BondA loan to a state or a companyInterest (coupon)Rising rates (the price falls), default, inflation
ETFA basket of many stocks or bondsWhatever the basket earnsThe market as a whole; the fund's fee
PropertyA building or landRent, appreciationLow liquidity, concentration, leverage through a mortgage
Trader

A horizon of minutes to days. Profits from the move. Many fast decisions.

Investor

A horizon of years to decades. Profits from the growth of the business and compound interest. Few slow decisions.

In common

Risk that is managed. Position size. Discipline. Records.

The mistake

Turning a losing trade into a "long-term investment" because you don't want to close it.

●39.2 ETFs: the simplest path

Most professional funds fail to beat their index over a long period after fees. That is why for most people the most sensible long-term investment is also the most boring: a broad, cheap index ETF, bought regularly, for years. The fee looks small, but it compounds.

Accumulating · натрупващAn ETF that reinvests dividends automatically.
Distributing · разпределящAn ETF that pays out dividends.

●●39.3 Two stock-picking methods

If you want to pick individual stocks, there are two approaches worth knowing. The first is American and looks for growth and momentum. The second is Bulgarian and looks for quality at a reasonable price.

Created by William O'Neil, founder of Investor's Business Daily, after studying the strongest stocks in American history. Each letter is a filter. In Bulgaria the method is taught in Simeon Petkov's trading courses.

LetterWhat it meansHow it is checked (roughly)
CCurrent earnings: current quarterly earningsGrowth in earnings per share versus a year earlier, often 20–25%+ is sought
AAnnual earnings: annual growthSteady earnings growth over several years
NNew: something newA new product, new management, a new price high
SSupply and demandA price rise on high volume
LLeader or laggardAmong the strongest in its sector
IInstitutional sponsorshipFunds are buying
MMarket directionThe whole market is in an uptrend

Note the M: even the best stock suffers when the market falls. It is the same idea as the macro bias in currencies: first the wind, then the boat.

MISARM is InvestClub's quick analysis method (Bozhidar Balevski), inspired by Warren Buffett and Benjamin Graham. The full course content is paid; here is only the public description: a quick analysis across four pillars.

PillarThe questionExample measure
PriceIs the company expensive relative to its earnings and sales?Price to earnings (P/E), price to sales
DebtCan it survive bad times?Debt to equity, interest coverage
EfficiencyHow well does it use its money?Return on equity, margins
Economic moatWhat stops competitors from taking its profit?Brand, network effect, switching costs, patents

The motto repeated in the courses: "Rule number one: never lose money. Rule number two: never forget rule number one." The same as 1% risk, only on a different horizon.

Not a recommendation

The two methods are given so you know they exist and how they think. Picking individual stocks takes separate study and, as with currencies, testing with data before real money.

StudentThen why trade currencies at all if an ETF is so simple?

MentorA good question, and not a rhetorical one. For most people an ETF is the better choice for most of their money. Trading is a skill, work, sometimes a passion. One does not rule out the other: long-term money in the ETF, risk capital in the skills. Just don't mix them.

Debrief

What you take from Dossier 39

  • Stocks, bonds, ETFs, property: different income, different risk.
  • Traders and investors work on different horizons. Don't turn a losing trade into an investment.
  • A broad, cheap ETF bought regularly is the sensible start for long-term money. Fees compound.
  • CANSLIM looks for growth and momentum; MISARM (the four pillars) looks for quality at a reasonable price.
  • Macro knowledge from currencies carries over directly.

Experienced Trader level · 1 scenario

No single right answer

You bought shares in a tech company for 500 euros as a swing trade with a mental stop 8% below entry, which you did not place in the platform. After weak earnings the stock gaps open 12% lower. You are thinking of holding it "long-term, because it's a good company". Rates are rising. You are in euros, and the stock is in dollars.

Task: what happened to the stop? Is this still a trade, or an investment? Which of the four pillars would you check if you really think long-term? What does the currency add?

Sample answer
  1. A mental stop closes nothing. Earnings come out outside exchange hours and the stock opens with a gap; even a placed stop would have filled at the first price, about −12% instead of −8% (Chapter 29).
  2. The trade had a swing plan and a stop. The decision to hold after price passed the stop is anchoring and loss aversion dressed up as an investment thesis. If you want an investment, open it separately, with a separate analysis and size.
  3. If you really analyse long-term: debt (rising rates make refinancing dearer), price (tech P/E is sensitive to rates), efficiency and moat. The weak report may be a signal about the moat.
  4. The result in euros also depends on EURUSD. If the dollar weakens, you lose on the currency too. The decision is yours, but the first rule is not to change horizon when a trade is losing.
