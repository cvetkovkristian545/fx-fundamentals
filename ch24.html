Basic Economics for the FX Trader

Economics is the study of the use of scarce resources which have alternative uses. Thomas Sowell, "Basic Economics"

After this chapter you will be able to

  • list the ten Sowell principles a trader uses every week, and where you meet them in the book;
  • trace a policy to the second and third stage and say what that means for the currency;
  • recognise a peg and capital controls as a price ceiling and measure the pressure with the parallel rate;
  • separate risk from uncertainty and read inflation as a hidden tax;
  • take apart the seven most common economic fallacies that cost pips.

●26.1 Why Sowell in a forex book

Thomas Sowell's "Basic Economics" is written without a single formula or a single chart. It is about how people think when their money is on the table. That is exactly why it is useful to a trader: the market is a place where millions of people with money on the table respond to incentives, prices and expectations. In Chapter 16 you saw the basics: scarcity, opportunity cost, incentives, prices as messages, comparative advantage. Here we gather them into one working kit and translate them into the language of currencies.

Sowell argues that most economic mistakes rest on three unstated assumptions. If you catch them in a headline, you already know where to be sceptical.

Assumption 1

Zero sum. "If one country gains from trade, the other loses." In FX this sounds like "Germany's exports are France's loss". Usually both sides gain, otherwise they would not trade.

Assumption 2

No competition. "Companies just raise prices because they are greedy." If that were so, they would raise them all the time. Prices move when demand, supply and costs move.

Assumption 3

No second stage. A policy is judged by its first effect and its intention. The market judges it by everything that follows.

●●26.2 Beyond stage one: five policies traced to the currency

Sowell asks every policy the same question: "And then what?" Stage one is the stated goal. Stage two is how people change their behaviour. Stage three is what remains after everyone has adjusted. The market starts pricing stage three on the day of stage one. Pick a policy.

  1. Stage 1 · the goal. The Ministry of Finance sells dollars and buys its own currency to stop the slide. The currency jumps sharply within minutes. Japan did exactly this in 2022 and 2024, with USDJPY between 145 and 162.
  2. Stage 2 · the behaviour. The rate differential has not changed. Holding dollars against yen still pays every day. Carry buyers come back lower, "at a discount".
  3. Stage 3 · what remains. The jump is erased within days or weeks unless the incentive changes. In July 2024 the Bank of Japan raised rates, US data weakened, and only then did the trend turn, with carry positions unwinding in early August.
  4. FX translation. An intervention buys time, it does not change the direction. Rates change it.
  1. Stage 1 · the goal. A central bank cuts rates while inflation is high, "to help growth".
  2. Stage 2 · the behaviour. The real rate turns deeply negative. Savers flee to dollars, gold, property. Importers buy currency in advance.
  3. Stage 3 · what remains. The currency weakens, imports get dearer, inflation speeds up. Turkey in 2021 is the textbook case: the central bank cut rates with inflation around 20%, and the lira lost about 44% against the dollar over the year.
  4. FX translation. Watch the real rate and trust, not the nominal cut.
  1. Stage 1 · the goal. Tariffs on imports "to protect jobs".
  2. Stage 2 · the behaviour. Firms that buy imported parts pay more. Partners retaliate with tariffs of their own. Supply chains are rearranged, investment waits for clarity.
  3. Stage 3 · what remains. By the textbook, fewer imports should strengthen the currency of the country imposing tariffs. In practice the trust channel can win: in spring 2025, after the US tariffs, the dollar weakened rather than strengthened, because the market priced uncertainty and weaker growth.
  4. FX translation. The same shock can move a currency in two directions. The price tells you which channel is winning at the moment.

  3. Stage 3 · what remains. Hidden inflation that comes out all at once when the cap goes, and a bigger deficit. In 2007 Zimbabwe ordered prices cut in half; the shops emptied.

  2. Stage 2 · the behaviour. People spend on goods while supply is limited. Prices rise.
  3. Stage 3 · what remains. Inflation, then rapid tightening. The US in 2020–2022: stimulus, inflation of 9.1% (June 2022), the fastest rate hikes since the 1980s and a strong dollar.
  4. FX translation. Fiscal stimulus today is often a rate differential tomorrow.
Myth

"Good intentions give good results."

Reality

The market does not read press releases about intentions. It reads what people will do when the incentives change. That is why in the bias file you write down stage 2, not the headline.

Tariff · митоA tax on imports.

●●26.3 The exchange rate is a price, and a peg is a ceiling

One of the strongest chapters in Sowell's book is on price controls. When the law holds a price below the market, the shortage does not disappear. It shows up as a queue, a black market, lower quality. The exchange rate is a price like any other. When the state declares an "official rate" stronger than the market and restricts buying dollars, a parallel rate is born: the price at which people actually exchange on the street or online.

The gap between the two rates is like a thermometer. It tells you how much pressure has built up behind the ceiling. Enter the two rates for the dollar.

●●26.4 Inflation as a hidden tax

Sowell writes that inflation is a tax nobody votes for. When the state finances its spending with new money, everyone who holds that currency pays. Not with a note from the tax office, but in that their money buys less. Those who pay most are the ones holding cash and fixed-rate savings.

For a trader the question is the same, but on the scale of the whole world: how much does it pay to hold this currency after inflation? That is the real rate. Capital looks for places where it is positive and stable.

StudentSo the currency with the highest rate always wins?

MentorOnly if the rate is above inflation and the market believes it will stay there. A 40% rate with 60% inflation is a negative real rate wearing an official suit. So compare the real rates of the two currencies in the pair, not the nominal ones.

Inflation tax · инфлационен данъкThe loss of money's purchasing power when the state finances spending by printing.
Purchasing power · покупателна способностHow many goods and services an amount buys.
Financial repression · финансова репресияRates held below inflation to melt away government debt at savers' expense.

●●26.5 A trade deficit is not a verdict

The words "favourable" and "unfavourable" trade balance are left over from the mercantilists, who believed wealth was the gold in the treasury. Sowell reminds us that Adam Smith refuted them back in 1776: wealth is goods and services. The US has run trade deficits almost every year since the 1970s and remains the richest large economy. Germany runs a permanent surplus and has had years of slow growth.

In accounting terms the balance of payments always balances. If a country imports more than it exports, the difference comes back as capital: foreigners buy its bonds, stocks, factories. So the right question is not "is there a deficit" but "who finances it, for how long and in what currency".

Mentor's note

The dollar is a special case. The world wants to hold dollar assets as reserves, so the US deficit is financed in its own currency. That is a privilege the Turkish lira or the South African rand do not have. The same deficit figure means different things for different currencies.

Current account · текуща сметкаTrade in goods and services plus income and transfers with the rest of the world.
Financial account · финансова сметкаPurchases and sales of assets between the country and the world. The mirror of the current account.
FDI · преки чуждестранни инвестицииLong-term investment in factories and companies.
Hot money · горещи париShort-term capital that moves quickly with rates and mood.
Mercantilism · меркантилизъмThe old idea that exports are wealth and imports a loss.

●●26.6 The speculator carries risk. Risk is not uncertainty

Sowell defends the speculator against their bad name, and his argument is useful for your self-respect and your discipline. Speculation does not create risk. It takes on risk that already exists. The farmer who sells his harvest in advance at a fixed price pays the speculator so he can sleep at night. The exporter hedging euros does the same. Sowell gives an example: a guaranteed 900 dollars a ton may be better for the farmer than an uncertain 1,000. The 100 difference is the price of peace of mind, and someone earns it because they take on the risk.

Gambling is the opposite: it creates risk that did not exist before. A trader with no edge, no stop and a size they cannot bear is not speculating. They are playing. The difference is not in the instrument but in the process.

The second idea comes from the chapter on time and risk. Risk can be calculated: you know when NFP is, you roughly know the distribution of surprises, you can choose a size. Uncertainty cannot: you do not know whether the rules will change tomorrow. Markets tolerate risk. Uncertainty paralyses them: investment is postponed, liquidity thins, spreads widen and technical levels weaken. Classify the events:

Speculation · спекулацияTaking on existing price risk in exchange for an expected profit.
Hedging · хеджиранеA trade that reduces the risk of another position. The hedger pays so as not to carry the risk.
Knightian uncertainty · несигурност по НайтThe unknown, to which no probability can be assigned. Distinct from measurable risk.
Policy uncertainty · политическа несигурностNot knowing what the rules, taxes or regulations will be tomorrow.

●26.7 The market lives in the future

Sowell compares two clocks. The politician looks as far as the next election. The market looks to infinity and translates the whole future into one price today: the present value. If a city freezes bus fares, the buses will fall apart in ten years, but the operator's share price falls today. The politician may no longer be in office when the bill comes. The market does not wait.

For currencies this means three things you have already met in the book:

  1. The price already contains what is expected. If the market prices a 90% chance of a rate cut, the cut itself barely moves the currency. The surprise moves it (Chapter 19).
  2. The currency reacts to the path, not today's rate. Forward guidance and the dot plot often move it more than the decision itself (Chapter 18).
  3. Trust is bought slowly and lost quickly. A central bank that has once betrayed expectations pays a premium for years (Chapter 27).
Myth

"The good news is already out, so now I'll buy."

Reality

If the news was expected, it was bought by those who expected it before it came out. You risk buying from them. Sowell would say that knowledge is the scarcest resource, and late knowledge is worth the least.

Present value · настояща стойностWhat an amount that will arrive in the future is worth today.
Priced in · заложено в ценатаAn expectation the market has already reflected in the rate.
Credibility · довериеThe market's belief that an institution will do what it says.

●●26.8 Seven fallacies that cost pips

The last chapter of "Basic Economics" is a list of the most common fallacies. Sowell quotes Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes: "think things, not words". In FX the most dangerous words are the ones that sound like a judgement: "strong", "weak", "deficit", "debt". Rate each statement.

Finally, everything in this chapter comes together in seven questions you can ask of every macro headline before it goes into your bias file.

#QuestionWhat you look for
1What incentives does this create?A change in the rate differential, in capital flows, in the behaviour of exporters and importers.
2What happens at stages 2 and 3?Who will adjust and how. Whether the effect will fade or grow.
3Is there a ceiling or a floor?A peg, interventions, controls. How big the gap with the market price is.
4Who pays the hidden tax?The real rate of the two currencies in the pair.
5Who finances the deficit?Long-term or hot money, own or foreign currency.
6Is it risk or uncertainty?An event with a date and a distribution, or a change of the rules. If the latter, smaller size.
7What is already in the price?Consensus, rate pricing, positioning. The trade is with the surprise.
Fallacy of composition · заблуда на композициятаAssuming that what is true for the part is true for the whole.
Crowded trade · претъпкана сделкаA position so many people have entered that the exit becomes narrow.
Strong currency · силна валутаAn expensive currency. Not a synonym for a strong economy.
Debrief

What you take from Dossier 26

  • Three assumptions sit behind most fallacies: zero sum, no competition, no second stage.
  • A policy is judged by its incentives and by stages 2 and 3. Intervention buys time, rates change the direction.
  • A peg and capital controls are a price ceiling. The gap with the parallel rate is the pressure that will come out all at once.
  • Inflation is a hidden tax. Compare real rates, not nominal ones.
  • A deficit is not a verdict. Ask who finances it, for how long and in what currency.
  • Speculation carries existing risk. Without an edge and a stop it is gambling. Uncertainty calls for smaller size.
  • The market lives in the future: the price contains what is expected, the surprise moves it.

No single right answer

A made-up G10 country with a currency at a 30-year low against the dollar. Its rate is 0.5%, the US rate is 4.5%, its inflation is 4%. On Thursday at 14:10 Bulgarian time the finance ministry intervenes and the currency gains 3% in 20 minutes. The central bank says it is "in no hurry" on rates. The same day the government announces a cap on household electricity prices, paid for from the budget.

Task: apply Sowell. What do the incentives say about how long the jump will last? What would change the direction at stage 3? What are the second-order effects of the price cap on inflation, the budget and the currency? How do you work on the day of the intervention and in the following weeks?

Sample answer
  1. The incentive has not changed: the differential is 4 pp in the dollar's favour, and the local currency's real rate is about −3.5%. Holding dollars against it still pays. The jump is more likely to be partly or fully erased within days or weeks, as in Japan in 2022 and 2024. The intervention buys time.
  2. The direction would be changed by a narrowing differential: the central bank hikes or signals a hike, or US data weaken and the market starts pricing Fed cuts. The strongest case is when both happen at once.
  3. The price cap suppresses measured inflation today, so the central bank has even less reason to hike. The budget pays the difference, the deficit grows. If the deficit is hard to finance, that is extra pressure on the currency. When the cap goes, the deferred inflation comes out all at once.
  4. On the day of the intervention spreads and moves are forced, not structural. It is sensible to wait, not to chase the jump and not to sell straight into it. In the following weeks I watch the differential and ministry statements and trade with reduced size while uncertainty is high. The decision whether and how to trade remains mine.
